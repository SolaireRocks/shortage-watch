Disease

Three views: whether anything looks like a pandemic, which communicable diseases are rising across the US, and what is going around in Ohio.
Disease briefing · 2026-11-28Elevated

Seasonal influenza activity is accelerating rapidly across the country and in Ohio, while novel avian flu indicators remain contained to direct agricultural exposures.

Current Situation

The composite Disease Index registers at 76.9, placing overall disease pressure in the elevated band. The primary driver is a rapid, early-season acceleration of seasonal influenza across the United States. Weekly nationwide influenza hospitalizations surged 81% over three weeks to 11,792 admissions, running more than 8,400 admissions above historical averages for late November. Clinical lab flu test positivity sits at 11.76%, while night coughing monitored via Sleep Cycle has risen to 9.37% of users. COVID-19 and RSV metrics show modest seasonal gains but remain below their respective multi-year historical averages.

In Ohio, trends parallel the national surge. Hospitalizations for influenza rose 126% over three weeks to 235 per week, and lab positivity reached 9.4%. Measles also remains elevated above 2025 levels in Ohio (238 cases year-to-date vs. 32 last year).

Pandemic Risk Assessment

Pandemic probabilities remain pinned close to historical base rates (0.1% at 1 week, 0.4% at 1 month, and 5.5% over 1 year). Although the World Health Organization reported three confirmed human H5N1 cases with one fatality among agricultural workers in Washington State, these cases stem from direct poultry contact. There is no evidence of secondary human-to-human transmission. Total US respiratory deaths (0.897 per 100,000) and overall acute respiratory emergency visits (14.48%) remain below seasonal benchmarks, confirming that current hospital strain reflects an early seasonal flu curve rather than an unmanaged novel outbreak.

What to Watch

Key indicators to monitor include whether the rapid growth in seasonal influenza admissions begins to strain regional hospital capacity, any genomic evidence of adaptive mutations or cluster spread in avian influenza cases, and whether winter COVID-19 or RSV waves compound current flu activity.

Novel pathogen surveillance is anchored by base rates, with the overall pandemic hazard remaining low. While the WHO confirmed three agricultural worker infections in Washington State with one fatality, all cases were linked to direct poultry exposure without evidence of secondary transmission. Commercial flock outbreaks dropped 9% over the past 30 days to 32 flocks, though wastewater samples showing H5 presence increased to 2.16%. Broader respiratory markers show no severe pathogen expansion: acute respiratory emergency department visits sit at 14.48% (1.89 percentage points below the multi-year seasonal norm), and combined pneumonia, influenza, and COVID-19 deaths remain low at 0.897 per 100,000. Surveillance continues to track animal-human interfaces closely.

From the 2026-11-28 disease briefingElevated

Seasonal influenza surges steeply nationwide while COVID-19 and RSV remain modest.

Weekly flu hospital admissions reached 11,792, up 81% over three weeks and well above typical levels. Emergency department visits attributed to flu climbed to 3.29%, while overnight coughing tracked by Sleep Cycle rose to 9.37%. Among notifiable conditions, indigenous measles cases remain elevated nationwide at 5,660 year-to-date, and cyclosporiasis totals are sharply above 2025 counts.

Ohio mirrors national trends with doubling flu admissions and positive tests.

In Ohio, respiratory illness is driven almost entirely by a steep influenza wave. Flu hospital admissions jumped 126% over three weeks to 235 admissions for the week ending November 14, running 159 admissions above the historical weekly average. Clinical lab positivity for flu climbed to 9.4%, up 123% over the prior three weeks, and emergency department flu visits reached 2.15%. COVID-19 and RSV metrics in the state remain comparatively mild, with RSV admissions at 20 weekly. Among state notifiable diseases, indigenous measles stands at 238 cases year-to-date compared to 32 at this point last year, warranting verification of family MMR vaccination status.
