Conflict and geopolitics · 2026-11-21Normal

Conflict risks hold steady while Strait of Hormuz traffic remains severely depressed at 2.3 transits per day.

Read the summary

Current Situation

The geopolitical risk index stands at 49.0, remaining squarely in the normal band. Worldwide conflict indicators show limited marginal change over the past week: NASA FIRMS reports zero listed energy or fertilizer infrastructure sites exhibiting unusual thermal anomalies, and monthly organized violence fatalities tracked by the Uppsala Conflict Data Program (UCDP) remain near baseline levels at 13,286 deaths for the latest reporting month. Recent reports from Crisis Group focus on localized violence in Sudan and South Sudan, which do not directly jeopardize major global export corridors for energy or agricultural inputs.

Chokepoint & Conflict Shock Outlook

The Strait of Hormuz remains effectively shuttered to standard commercial maritime traffic. The latest reading from IMF PortWatch records only 2.0 daily transits, yielding a 7-day moving average of 2.3 ships per day. Because resolution requires a 7-day average exceeding 60 transits per day, a rapid reopening within one month remains exceptionally unlikely (2.5%). While Polymarket's aggregated one-month anchor shows 26.5%, this figure is distorted by an illiquid mid-December contract, whereas the December 31 contract sits near 2.9%. Longer horizons reflect gradually increasing odds of eventual diplomatic or naval resolution (35% within six months; 55% within a year).

For a new major conflict shock involving a top-five commodity exporter, probabilities remain closely anchored to historical baselines: 0.9% across one month and 11% over the next year. Prediction markets price the likelihood of a Chinese blockade of Taiwan before year-end at 2.35%, reflecting low short-term escalation risk.

What to Watch

Key metrics to monitor include IMF PortWatch daily transit numbers through the Strait of Hormuz, maritime insurance rate adjustments in the Persian Gulf, and thermal surveillance around critical export terminals.

Current Assessment

The broad economy remains in the normal range, with our aggregate economic index standing at 28.5 out of 100. Core underlying indicators show steady stability across employment and broader economic activity. The real-time Sahm rule indicator registers at 0.0 points, comfortably beneath the 0.5-point threshold that historically marks the start of an economic downturn. In addition, weather- and weekday-adjusted US grid electricity demand is running 0.788% above baseline expectations over the past four weeks, signalling resilient commercial and industrial activity.

Financial markets reflect some selective nervousness. The VIX volatility index registered at 28.44, driving a modest 7-day uptick of 5.8 points in the overall risk score. High-yield credit spreads closed at 3.52 points. However, broader financial plumbing remains accommodated, with the Chicago Fed Financial Conditions Index reading -0.478 and the 10-year minus 3-month Treasury yield curve sitting positively sloped at 0.60 points.

Outlook and Forecasts

We assess the near-term probability of entering a recession as low: 0.3% over the next week, 1.3% over one month, and 2.5% over three months. These near-term odds closely match prediction markets, which price a three-month recession risk at roughly 2.8%. Looking out to the six-month and one-year horizons, we assign probabilities of 11% and 26%, respectively. The 1-year estimate is in line with consensus market indicators (28.3%), capturing standard late-cycle vulnerabilities while recognizing that hard macroeconomic data has not deteriorated.

Key Indicators to Monitor

We will watch for any deterioration in labor metrics via updates to the Sahm rule, persistent widening in high-yield credit spreads beyond 4.5 points, or an inversion of the yield curve back into negative territory.

Read the summary

The Disease index stands at 67.0 today, placing it in the elevated band primarily due to early-season surges in typical winter respiratory pathogens and sustained climatic anomalies. Emergency department visits attributed to influenza have climbed to 2.48% of total visits (stress score 3.13), while wastewater surveillance indicates moderate viral activity for both SARS-CoV-2 (median viral activity level of 6.345) and influenza A (3.03). Simultaneously, NOAA's Oceanic Niño Index indicates a strong El Niño pattern with an anomaly of 2.46°C, which factors into systemic environmental disease tracking.

Despite the elevated composite index, the forward-looking probability of a true pandemic event—defined as a WHO-declared Public Health Emergency of International Concern for a novel pathogen or confirmed sustained human-to-human transmission of H5N1 in the United States—remains anchored near historical base rates. Historically, major novel-pathogen emergencies occur roughly once every twenty years (such as 2009 H1N1 and 2020 COVID-19), establishing a baseline annualized probability around 5%. Today's near-term probabilities are assessed at 0.1% over one week, 0.4% over one month, 1.3% over three months, 2.6% over six months, and 5.5% over the full year.

Recent global surveillance reports, such as four locally acquired chikungunya cases reported by Swiss authorities in Ticino, represent localized vector-borne dynamics rather than systemic respiratory threats. We continue to monitor domestic H5N1 poultry and dairy surveillance alongside emergency department syndromic signals. A material upward revision in pandemic probability would require evidence of secondary and tertiary viral transmission among humans or genetic adaptations signaling efficient airborne transmission in mammals.

Infrastructure risk remains elevated on persistent cyber exploitation activity, though physical hazards and space weather remain quiet.

Read the summary

Current Infrastructure Posture

The infrastructure and natural hazards composite index sits at 64.5, maintaining an elevated posture after a 5.0-point weekly rise. The primary source of pressure remains concentrated in the cyber domain. The Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA) has tracked 36 newly exploited vulnerabilities over the past 30 days, representing an elevated stress score of 1.91 standard deviations above baseline. Additionally, one actively exploited flaw in industrial control systems has been cataloged over the trailing 90 days.

Recent law enforcement operations highlight the sustained adversarial focus on critical domestic supply chains. International authorities disrupted a ransomware network in Ukraine extorting US energy and healthcare entities, while US prosecutors secured extraditions of affiliates involved in hospital ransomware campaigns. While enforcement helps degrade operational capacity, threat actors continue targeting systemic bottlenecks such as pipelines, hospital systems, and pharmacy claims aggregators.

Physical and Space Hazards

Physical and space weather indicators remain quiescent. Space Weather Prediction Center readings show a maximum planetary K-index of 2.67, indicating quiet geomagnetic conditions well below the threshold (Kp 8+) that strains long-distance electrical transmission networks. Global seismic activity registered five magnitude 6.5+ earthquakes over the past month, with no significant operational fallout for domestic ports or distribution corridors.

Probability Outlook

Historically, severe systemic infrastructure failures—such as the 2021 Colonial Pipeline shutdown, Winter Storm Uri, or the 2024 Change Healthcare outage—occur roughly once every 4 to 5 years (a 20% annual base rate). Current elevated software vulnerability pressure and active targeting keep the 1-week probability at 1.5% and the 1-month probability at 5.0%, slightly above historical base rates (0.43% and 1.82%, respectively). In the absence of an imminent severe weather system or an active operational shutdown, no extraordinary household actions are recommended beyond routine preparedness.
