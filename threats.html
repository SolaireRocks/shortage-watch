Geopolitical risk indicators remain steady as Strait of Hormuz transit numbers stay near standstill.

Current Situation

The Conflict and Geopolitics index stands at 49.0, remaining firmly in the normal band with negligible change over the past week. Satellite monitoring from NASA FIRMS detected zero abnormal thermal anomalies at key global energy and fertilizer infrastructure sites as of November 28. UCDP conflict fatalities worldwide remain consistent with historical baselines. While an automated airspace heuristic flagged reduced flight activity over the Black Sea, control boxes remained stable and there are no active verified event escalations.

Why These Probabilities?

  • Strait of Hormuz Reopening: The definition requires the IMF PortWatch 7-day average transit count to cross back above 60 ships per day. As of November 22, transits registered at 1.0 ship per day with a 7-day average of 2.7 ships per day. Given the severe operational disruption, a rapid resumption within one week (0.3%) or one month (3.0%) is highly improbable. Over longer horizons, resolution chances gradually increase to 30.0% by six months and 48.0% by one year.
  • New Conflict Shock: A conflict shock is defined as a brand-new war or blockade involving a top-five global exporter of oil, gas, grain, or fertilizer (or a Taiwan blockade), distinct from existing hostilities. Historically, such shocks occur roughly once every ten years (~10% annual base rate). With satellite indicators calm and no immediate troop mobilizations targeting new major exporters, the 1-year probability is estimated near the base rate at 11.0%.

What to Watch

We monitor daily PortWatch vessel counts through Hormuz for early signs of commercial convoy restoration, NASA FIRMS thermal alerts at primary processing terminals, and maritime corridor status across the Black Sea and Persian Gulf.

Economic Outlook

Macroeconomic Data vs. Market Volatility

Real-economy metrics currently show no signs of imminent contraction. The real-time Sahm rule reading remains at 0.0 points, well clear of the 0.5-point trigger that historically marks the onset of recessions. The Treasury yield curve (10-year minus 3-month) is positively sloped at +0.59 points, providing a supportive backdrop. Over a 12-month horizon, the estimated risk rises to 26%, slightly below the prediction market consensus of 29%. This reflects standard mid-cycle vulnerability to unexpected exogenous shocks rather than active deterioration today.

Key indicators to monitor over the coming weeks include labor market releases affecting the Sahm rule, persistent widening in corporate credit spreads, and any sharp shift in financial conditions that could curtail lending.

Read the summary

Current Situation

The composite Disease Index registers at 76.9, placing overall disease pressure in the elevated band. The primary driver is a rapid, early-season acceleration of seasonal influenza across the United States. Weekly nationwide influenza hospitalizations surged 81% over three weeks to 11,792 admissions, running more than 8,400 admissions above historical averages for late November. Clinical lab flu test positivity sits at 11.76%, while night coughing monitored via Sleep Cycle has risen to 9.37% of users. COVID-19 and RSV metrics show modest seasonal gains but remain below their respective multi-year historical averages.

In Ohio, trends parallel the national surge. Hospitalizations for influenza rose 126% over three weeks to 235 per week, and lab positivity reached 9.4%. Measles also remains elevated above 2025 levels in Ohio (238 cases year-to-date vs. 32 last year).

Pandemic Risk Assessment

Pandemic probabilities remain pinned close to historical base rates (0.1% at 1 week, 0.4% at 1 month, and 5.5% over 1 year). Although the World Health Organization reported three confirmed human H5N1 cases with one fatality among agricultural workers in Washington State, these cases stem from direct poultry contact. There is no evidence of secondary human-to-human transmission. Total US respiratory deaths (0.897 per 100,000) and overall acute respiratory emergency visits (14.48%) remain below seasonal benchmarks, confirming that current hospital strain reflects an early seasonal flu curve rather than an unmanaged novel outbreak.

What to Watch

Key indicators to monitor include whether the rapid growth in seasonal influenza admissions begins to strain regional hospital capacity, any genomic evidence of adaptive mutations or cluster spread in avian influenza cases, and whether winter COVID-19 or RSV waves compound current flu activity.
