Economic Outlook

The overall economic stress index sits at 30.0, placing conditions squarely within the normal band. Underlying fundamental indicators show substantial resilience even as short-term financial volatility has ticked upward.

Macroeconomic Data vs. Market Volatility

Real-economy metrics currently show no signs of imminent contraction. The real-time Sahm rule reading remains at 0.0 points, well clear of the 0.5-point trigger that historically marks the onset of recessions. The Treasury yield curve (10-year minus 3-month) is positively sloped at +0.59 points, providing a supportive backdrop. Weather-adjusted electricity grid demand is steady at +0.729% above expected levels, reflecting healthy industrial and commercial baseload activity. Furthermore, the Chicago Fed National Financial Conditions Index remains loose at -0.444.

In contrast, financial market measures show moderate stress. The VIX volatility index stands at 26.8, and high-yield credit spreads are slightly elevated at 3.77 points. These readings indicate investor nervousness rather than broad balance-sheet distress across the economy, as non-financial credit channels remain functioning without widespread contraction.

Probabilities and What to Watch

Near-term recession risk remains closely anchored to baseline levels, with a 0.3% probability over 1 week and 3.0% over 3 months, aligning with prediction market sentiment (2.9% average across Kalshi and Polymarket). Over a 12-month horizon, the estimated risk rises to 26%, slightly below the prediction market consensus of 29%. This reflects standard mid-cycle vulnerability to unexpected exogenous shocks rather than active deterioration today.

Key indicators to monitor over the coming weeks include labor market releases affecting the Sahm rule, persistent widening in corporate credit spreads, and any sharp shift in financial conditions that could curtail lending.

What would change this view

  • A rapid climb in the real-time Sahm rule toward 0.5 points indicating rising unemployment.
  • A re-inversion of the 10-year minus 3-month Treasury yield curve alongside widening high-yield spreads past 5.0 points.
  • A sharp tightening in the Chicago Fed National Financial Conditions Index into positive territory.
