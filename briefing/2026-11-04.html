Current Situation

The Shortage Probability Index (SPI) sits at 64.2 as of November 4, 2026, keeping overall risk in the elevated band. Fuel remains the primary source of supply stress, driven by ongoing maritime disruption in the Strait of Hormuz and substantial price pressure across petroleum products. Retail diesel stands at $6.292 per gallon (+67.7% year-over-year) and regular gasoline at $4.153 per gallon (+37.6% year-over-year), while Brent crude trades at $107.03 per barrel. Despite high pump costs and freight pressure, physical US fuel distribution infrastructure continues to function without widespread station outages or regional rationing.

Food, natural gas, and fertilizer indicators remain subdued domestically. Natural gas hard data indicates stable conditions (index -0.03), and fertilizer hard data is neutral (0.00), despite isolated increases such as a 6% rise in UAN28. Global staple agricultural commodities show notable year-over-year increases—wheat at $317/mt (+37.3%) and rice at $489/mt (+37.4%)—but domestic grocery shelves remain well-stocked, and no US retailer purchase limits have been observed.

Why These Probabilities

Historical base rates demonstrate that genuine physical retail shortages in the United States are rare events, typically precipitated by extreme natural disasters (such as hurricanes disabling Gulf Coast refineries) or acute infrastructure sabotage. While current fuel prices represent a substantial cost shock, high prices alone do not constitute a physical stockout under our event definitions.

Accordingly, fuel shortage probabilities remain elevated relative to base rates—at 4% over 1 week and 15% over 1 month, rising to 45% over 1 year—reflecting low near-term odds of Strait of Hormuz transit normalization and depleted Strategic Petroleum Reserve buffers (282.2 million barrels, down 31.1% YoY). However, they stay well below headline sentiment because domestic refining and delivery infrastructure remain operational. Food (1.5% at 1 month), natural gas (1.5% at 1 month), and fertilizer (1.5% at 1 month) are kept near baseline levels given ample physical supplies.

Stocking Guidance

Stocking guidance remains at baseline. Households should maintain a standard emergency pantry containing 3 to 14 days of shelf-stable food, drinking water, and essential household supplies. Preemptive stockpiling is not warranted by the data, as grocery shelves and consumer fuel distribution networks remain open and functioning normally nationwide.

What would change this view

  • A confirmed multi-week reopening of commercial shipping through the Strait of Hormuz with transits exceeding 60 vessels daily, which would sharply lower fuel disruption probabilities.
  • Widespread physical terminal outages or multi-state fuel delivery shortfalls lasting longer than 72 hours in the US.
  • Spillover from elevated diesel logistics costs into multi-state retail grocery limits or rapid month-over-month jumps in CPI food at home above 2%.
