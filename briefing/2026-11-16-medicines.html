Supply Conditions and Telehealth Expiration Outlook

Overall prescription drug availability remains stable across the United States. The Pharmaceutical Scarcity Index (PSI) registered 26.6 as of November 16, comfortably within the normal range and down 1.7 points over the last 30 days. Active drug shortages tracked by the American Society of Health-System Pharmacists (ASHP) stood at 225 at the end of the third quarter of 2026, remaining well below the threshold of 300 and the 2024 peak of 323. Generic price-shock breadth is negligible at 0.8%, and the FDA shortage database lists 68 active items with only one addition (Pentostatin Injection) in the past 90 days. Consequently, broad pharmaceutical shortage probabilities track historic baseline rates.

However, regulatory access risks remain elevated. Federal telemedicine flexibilities allowing remote prescribing of Schedule II controlled substances without an initial in-person examination are scheduled to lapse on December 31, 2026—just 45 days away. With no final DEA special registration rule published, the 3-month probability of a qualifying access disruption remains elevated at 0.25.
