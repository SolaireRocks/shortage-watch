Current Situation

The Shortage Pressure Index (SPI) is at 73.0, remaining in the elevated band. The primary source of system strain remains the petroleum and fuels complex (hard data index D = 1.50). US retail diesel sits at $6.13 per gallon—up 59.7% year-over-year—driven by wide refining crack spreads and global crude pressures, with Brent crude trading at $107.03 per barrel (+62.7% YoY) and WTI at $99.64 per barrel (+61.3% YoY). The upstream bottleneck remains persistent: IMF PortWatch data shows Strait of Hormuz maritime transits averaging just 3.9 commercial vessels per day, far below normal traffic.

In contrast, natural gas (D = -0.03) and fertilizer (D = 0.00) show stable domestic conditions. Food supply indicators register moderate international pressure (D = 0.69), driven by higher international grain prices (wheat up 37.3% YoY and rice up 37.4% YoY). However, domestic growing conditions are stable, with contiguous US drought covering 41.7% of the area (6.4 percentage points below the five-year average), and domestic grocery price increases remain steady around 0.5% monthly rather than exhibiting crisis-level spikes.

Assessment and Probabilities

Historical base rates demonstrate that nationwide shortages or formal retail purchase limits are rare outside of severe hurricanes, sudden pipeline shutdowns, or wartime embargoes. While current crude and diesel prices are sharply elevated, US distribution infrastructure continues to move freight and fuel without widespread physical dry-outs. As a result, 1-week and 1-month fuel event risks are assessed at 4% and 15% respectively, reflecting primarily the vulnerability of regional distribution to sudden secondary shocks while inventories and the SPR (282.2 million barrels, down 31.1% YoY) remain lean.

Food shortage risk remains low at 0.3% over 1 week and 1.5% over 1 month, as US logistics networks and retail grocery channels show normal operational stocking without hoarding behavior. Natural gas and fertilizer show low near-term disruption probabilities (1.5% over 1 month), reflecting comfortable seasonal positioning.

What to Watch

Key indicators to monitor over the next fortnight include Strait of Hormuz tanker transit counts, weekly EIA diesel inventory draws, and freight rate pass-through into grocery transportation costs.

What would change this view

  • Strait of Hormuz daily commercial transits returning toward normal baseline levels (>60 per day), which would substantially lower fuel risk.
  • Widespread fuel delivery failures or multi-state station outages exceeding 3 days, which would trigger an immediate jump in short-term fuel outage probabilities.
  • A sudden acceleration of US retail food CPI above 2% in a single month driven by diesel freight surcharges.

What to do

  • Prepare Fuel (gasoline, diesel)
    Keep primary vehicle fuel tanks above half capacity to buffer against localized distributor delays and retail price volatility driven by high diesel crack spreads.
