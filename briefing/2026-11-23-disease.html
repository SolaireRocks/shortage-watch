The overall Disease risk posture sits at elevated, driven predominantly by a rapid, unseasonably early escalation in seasonal influenza activity rather than any emerging novel pathogen threat.

Current Trajectory

Across the United States, influenza transmission is accelerating sharply. Hospital admissions for confirmed influenza reached 11,792 for the week ending November 14, 2026—a figure 8,437 admissions higher than the five-year average for mid-November. Laboratory test positivity has reached 11.755%, and wastewater concentrations of influenza A have more than tripled over the past three weeks. In Ohio, influenza admissions jumped 126% over the same interval to 235 weekly admissions, alongside a 9.4% test positivity rate. Other common respiratory pathogens remain relatively subdued: COVID-19 hospitalizations (5,025 nationally; 160 in Ohio) and RSV activity are tracking near or below multi-year averages for late autumn, keeping overall emergency department utilization for acute respiratory illness (13.34% nationally; 14.35% in Ohio) near expected levels.

Why These Probabilities

Pandemic probabilities remain pinned close to historical baseline rates (0.1% over one week, 0.4% over one month, and 5.5% over one year). An elevated seasonal flu surge does not constitute a pandemic under our criteria, which require a WHO Public Health Emergency of International Concern (PHEIC) for a novel agent or confirmed sustained human-to-human transmission of H5N1 in the United States. Although avian influenza H5 detections in wastewater rose to 2.16% of samples and 27 flocks reported outbreaks over the past month, there is no signal of efficient human spread or unlinked clinical clusters.

What to Watch

Over the coming weeks, key signals to monitor include whether the influenza wave peaks early or strains regional hospital capacity, whether wastewater H5 markers correlate with any human case clusters, and the ongoing trajectory of notifiable conditions like measles.

What would change this view

  • Genetic sequencing demonstrating sustained human-to-human transmission of avian influenza A(H5N1) in the United States.
  • A cluster of novel respiratory infections prompting WHO to declare a Public Health Emergency of International Concern (PHEIC).

Pandemic-level threat

Normal

Avian influenza surveillance shows ongoing animal spillover but zero sustained human transmission.

Indicators for novel pandemic-grade pathogens remain anchored near historical baselines. Avian influenza surveillance detected H5 markers in 2.16% of wastewater samples, a 96% increase over three weeks, alongside 27 confirmed commercial or backyard flock infections (3.71 million birds culled over 30 days). However, preprint research activity on novel outbreaks remains flat (log ratio -0.001 vs 90-day baseline), and broader surveillance shows no novel clusters. Total acute respiratory emergency department visits sit at 13.34% (-2.31 percentage points below typical seasonal averages), and combined mortality from pneumonia, influenza, and COVID-19 is 0.893 per 100,000 per week, below multi-year averages. Without evidence of sustained mammalian adaptation or human-to-human chains, pandemic risk remains at normal background expectation.

Other communicable diseases

Elevated

Seasonal influenza activity is expanding rapidly across the United States weeks ahead of usual winter timing.

Seasonal influenza is driving the primary communicable disease burden nationwide. Weekly influenza hospital admissions reached 11,792 (an 81% increase over three weeks and 8,437 above historical expectations for mid-November, registering a maximum stress score of 4.0). Clinical laboratory test positivity climbed to 11.755% (+6.79 points above typical benchmarks), and wastewater influenza A viral activity rose 203% over three weeks to 3.03. In contrast, COVID-19 hospital admissions remain subdued at 5,025 per week (-14,551 vs historical comparisons), and RSV activity is quiet (emergency visits at 0.16%). Among notifiable conditions, cyclosporiasis (33,567 YTD vs 4,163 last year) and indigenous measles (5,224 YTD vs 1,613 last year) continue to show significant cumulative multi-year elevations.

Ohio

Elevated

In Ohio, respiratory illness is dominated by a sharp early surge in influenza. Weekly flu hospital admissions reached 235, up 126% over three weeks and 159.2 admissions above historical mid-November baselines. Ohio clinical lab test positivity for flu climbed to 9.4% (up 123% over three weeks, +4.68 points above usual), and emergency department visits for influenza reached 1.47%. Broader respiratory stress remains manageable: total acute respiratory emergency visits (14.35%) and outpatient ILINet visits (2.354%) sit slightly below typical seasonal levels. COVID-19 admissions remain low at 160 per week. State notifiable disease tracking shows indigenous measles running elevated year-to-date (218 cases vs 32 at this point last year), alongside 6,754 cyclosporiasis cases.
