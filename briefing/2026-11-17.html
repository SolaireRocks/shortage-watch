Current Situation

The Shortage Pressure Index (SPI) sits at 60.6, placing overall supply risk at the lower boundary of the elevated band. While the index has cooled by 7.6 points over the last 30 days, persistent energy pressures continue to generate elevated risks in the fuel domain. Retail diesel remains elevated at $6.127 per gallon (+59.7% year-over-year), and retail gasoline is at $3.995 per gallon (+30.7% year-over-year). Upstream geopolitical stress remains focused on the Persian Gulf, where Strait of Hormuz commercial transits are averaging just 2.3 vessels per day—well below normal baseline operations.

Despite high pump prices, domestic physical distribution remains functional. Commercial gasoline inventories are tracking 0.6% above their 5-year seasonal average, preventing acute, regional outages at local filling stations. Upstream crude prices (Brent at $92.14/bbl, WTI at $89.62/bbl) and high refining margins account for the elevated fuel index rather than physical stock exhaustion.

Why These Probabilities?

  • Fuel (1-month: 15%, 1-year: 45%): Fuel faces the highest disruption probability among monitored sectors. Depressed Hormuz transits and a depleted Strategic Petroleum Reserve (279.5 million barrels, down 31.9% YoY) leave the domestic market sensitive to secondary supply shocks or refinery outages. However, the short-term 1-week probability remains low at 4.0% because domestic inventories are adequate and terminal throughput is stable.
  • Food (1-month: 1.5%, 1-year: 12%): Global agricultural prices remain significantly higher than last year (world wheat up 37.3%, rice up 37.4%), and spot freight rates spiked over 50% year-over-year, contributing to retail grocery inflation. Nevertheless, US drought conditions have improved (-6.4 points vs 5-year average), domestic harvest logistics are functioning, and grocery shelf stocking remains orderly.
  • Natural Gas & Fertilizer (1-month: 1.5% each): Natural gas hard data (-0.05) and retail fertilizer indicators (0.01) remain near seasonal norms. Current weather patterns have not placed abnormal strain on pipeline networks or heating deliveries.

What to Watch

Over the next few weeks, key indicators to monitor include changes in Strait of Hormuz tanker transit counts, early winter temperature anomalies that could stress natural gas deliverability, and freight spot rate trends as retail holiday shipping peaks. Household action remains at the baseline level: maintain standard home emergency provisions of shelf-stable food and water, with no need for speculative purchasing or hoarding.

What would change this view

  • A recovery of Strait of Hormuz commercial traffic above 60 daily transits, which would rapidly ease crude and distillate crack spreads.
  • A multi-state refined fuel pipeline shutdown or refinery outage that rapidly cuts pump supply and drives a 25% price increase within four weeks.
  • Early winter extreme freezes triggering widespread Henry Hub natural gas price spikes above twice their 1-year median.
