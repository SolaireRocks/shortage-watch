Why These Probabilities

The baseline risk for a novel global pandemic is historically rare, occurring roughly once every two decades (such as 2009 H1N1 and 2020 COVID-19), corresponding to an annualized baseline probability of approximately 5%. The forecast definition requires either a WHO declaration of a Public Health Emergency of International Concern (PHEIC) for a novel agent or confirmed sustained human-to-human spread of H5N1 within the United States. With no active event proposals, no anomalous unclassified outbreak clusters, and no sustained human transmission of avian influenza detected, forward-looking probabilities remain pegged to historical base rates: 0.1% over one week, 0.4% over one month, 1.3% over three months, 2.6% over six months, and 5.5% over the full year.
