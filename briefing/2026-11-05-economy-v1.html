Current Assessment

The overall economic stress index stands at 41.8, comfortably within the normal band below 60. Although the index has risen slightly by 3.2 points over the past week and 10.2 points over the past 30 days, underlying macroeconomic data reflects steady baseline conditions. The primary real-time recession monitor, the Sahm rule, remains at 0.0 points—well beneath the 0.5-point trigger that historically marks the start of a downturn.

Financial liquidity and credit markets show mild, manageable tension rather than systemic stress. High-yield credit spreads closed at 3.4 points, carrying a moderate stress score of 1.17, while the VIX volatility index registered 20.83. Broader financial conditions remain accommodative: the Chicago Fed National Financial Conditions Index recorded -0.459 as of late October, confirming that overall credit availability remains loose. Industrial activity proxies, such as weather- and weekday-adjusted US grid demand (+0.139% versus expected), point to stable commercial electricity utilization.

Forecast Outlook

Short-term recession risk remains aligned with baseline probabilities: 0.3% over one week, 1.3% over one month, and 4.0% over three months. Looking further out, the six-month probability is evaluated at 12%, and the one-year risk is estimated at 25%. This 12-month estimate aligns closely with prediction market consensus, where Polymarket (30.0%) and Kalshi (21.5%) average 25.8% for a recession by late 2027.

Household Guidance

No emergency adjustments or defensive spending freezes are warranted at this time. Standard household financial prudence—maintaining an adequate emergency cash reserve and managing discretionary debts—remains sufficient. Key series to track for potential inflection include future Sahm rule releases and any rapid widening in corporate credit spreads.

What would change this view

  • A sharp spike in the Real-time Sahm rule toward 0.5 accompanied by sudden deterioration in weekly initial jobless claims.
  • A widening of high-yield credit spreads beyond 5.0 points alongside a sustained rise in the Chicago Fed financial conditions index above zero.
