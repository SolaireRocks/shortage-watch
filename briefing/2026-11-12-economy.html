Macroeconomic Assessment

The composite Economy stress index sits at 21.7, well within the normal band (below 60) and down 15.9 points over the past week. Core indicators show no signs of an imminent economic downturn. The Real-time Sahm rule stands at 0.0 points, far beneath the 0.5-point threshold that traditionally signals the start of a recession.

Broader financial and activity indicators corroborate this stability. The Chicago Fed National Financial Conditions Index is negative at -0.462, confirming that credit and financial conditions remain loose. High-yield credit spreads are contained at 3.3 points, the VIX volatility index is subdued at 17.49, and the 10-year minus 3-month Treasury yield curve is positively sloped at +0.68 points. Real industrial activity also shows steady output, with four-week weather- and weekday-adjusted US electrical grid demand tracking 0.351% above expectations.

Forecast & Risk Horizons

Given the strength in labor and credit data, near-term recession probabilities remain minimal: 0.3% over 1 week, 1.3% over 1 month, and 3.0% over 3 months. Over the 3-month horizon, our estimate is consistent with prediction markets, where Polymarket (2.2%) and Kalshi (1.5%) imply minimal risk of a downturn in late 2026.

Looking further out, the 6-month (11%) and 1-year (24%) probabilities factor in normal late-cycle macroeconomic risks. The 1-year forecast aligns with the crowd consensus across Polymarket (28%) and Kalshi (20.5%), representing a moderate premium above historical 1-year base rates (15%) to reflect standard cyclical uncertainty.

What to Watch

We continue to monitor the monthly Sahm rule readings for any labor market softening, shifts in the 10-year minus 3-month Treasury spread back into inversion, and widening in corporate bond spreads.

What would change this view

  • The Real-time Sahm rule rising rapidly toward the 0.5 trigger point.
  • A sudden widening of high-yield credit spreads beyond 5.0 points.
  • A sharp deterioration in weather-adjusted electricity demand indicating industrial contraction.
