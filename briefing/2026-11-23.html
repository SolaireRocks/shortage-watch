Current Outlook: Elevated Vulnerability in Fuels

The Shortage Pressure Index (SPI) stands at 64.2, placing overall domestic supply risks in the elevated band. While widespread physical availability of everyday staples remains intact, energy markets continue to carry the bulk of current systemic stress.

Energy and Fuels

Fuel remains the primary area of concern. Retail diesel stands at $5.937 per gallon (up 53.5% year-over-year), while retail regular gasoline is at $3.805 per gallon (up 24.3% year-over-year). Underlying crude benchmarks reflect continued tension, with Brent at $92.94/bbl and WTI at $89.53/bbl. On the supply side, US commercial gasoline stocks are comfortable at 2.6% above their five-year seasonal average, but distillate inventories are 6.8% below seasonal averages and the Strategic Petroleum Reserve remains low at 276.4 million barrels. Given that Strait of Hormuz transits remain stalled at roughly 2.3 transits per day, the fuel 1-month shortage risk is assessed at 15% and 3-month risk at 28%, primarily reflecting the probability of a qualifying 25% price spike rather than physical terminal runouts.

Food and Agricultural Inputs

Domestic retail grocery networks remain stable. The food shortage probability is steady at 1.5% at 1 month and 4.0% at 3 months. Although international commodity grains reflect cost pressures—with global wheat and rice up approximately 37% year-over-year—and domestic freight spot rates show holiday season tightening, these pressures translate to persistent grocery inflation rather than missing items or rationing. In the fertilizer domain, retail prices for five of eight major fertilizers nudged higher, but hard data indicators show minimal physical distribution distress (hard data index at 0.01).

Natural Gas

Natural gas hard data index sits near neutral (0.02). With Henry Hub pricing orderly and early winter heating inventories balanced, near-term disruption risk to residential delivery remains low at 1.5% over 1 month and 4.0% over 3 months.

Household Strategy

No general emergency stockpiling is warranted. Standard baseline emergency pantry practices (maintaining several days to two weeks of staple food and water) remain appropriate. Households should maintain routine vehicle fueling habits, keeping tanks above half full to ride out short-term price swings.

What would change this view

  • A sustained resumption of Strait of Hormuz commercial traffic above 60 transits per day, which would significantly cool global crude benchmarks.
  • A rapid multi-week jump in national retail gasoline prices exceeding 25%, or widespread refinery outages triggering multi-state terminal runouts.
  • A sharp spike in domestic Henry Hub spot prices to double their 1-year median ahead of severe winter freezes.

What to do

  • Prepare Fuel (gasoline, diesel)
    Keep primary vehicles filled above half a tank during routine travel to buffer against ongoing price volatility and tight regional diesel and distillate supplies.
