The overall Economy risk index registers at 21.3, well within the normal band, having declined by 10.2 points over the past week and 8.6 points over the past month. Across major macro and financial indicators, hard data continues to reflect an expanding economy without visible markers of imminent contraction.

The Real-time Sahm rule, our primary trigger for turning-point recession calls, stands at 0.0 points as of the latest reading. This leaves a comfortable margin before reaching the 0.5 threshold that historically indicates the onset of a downturn. Labor market and activity signals are backed by benign financial conditions: the Chicago Fed National Financial Conditions Index is at -0.462, signaling ample liquidity and below-average friction in domestic credit. While high-yield credit spreads notched a mild stress score of 0.83, the absolute level of 3.3 points remains modest and consistent with orderly corporate financing. Equity volatility is similarly contained, with the VIX at 17.49, and four-week weather-adjusted US electricity grid demand is running 0.386% above seasonal expectations, reflecting stable industrial and commercial baseload activity.

Near-term recession probabilities remain pinned close to structural base rates, sitting at 0.3% over one week, 1.3% over one month, and 3.0% over three months, aligned with prediction market anchors on Polymarket and Kalshi. At the 12-month horizon, we maintain a 24.0% probability, factoring in medium-term cyclical risks and potential monetary policy lags, slightly below the Kalshi-Polymarket blend of 25.0%.

For households, no emergency precautions or balance-sheet retrenchments are warranted today. Standard financial management—preserving an emergency cash cushion and avoiding high-interest debt—remains sufficient. We will continue tracking monthly employment prints, credit spread movements, and high-frequency production indicators for early signs of strain.

What would change this view

  • A sudden spike in the Sahm rule toward 0.5 driven by rapid deterioration in monthly payrolls and rising unemployment.
  • A widening of high-yield spreads above 5.0 points alongside sharp tightening in the Chicago Fed financial conditions index.
  • Two consecutive quarters of negative real GDP growth.
