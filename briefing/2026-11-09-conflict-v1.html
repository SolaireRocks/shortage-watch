Current Situation

The geopolitical risk index sits at 47.3, holding within the normal band, though 12 of 14 underlying indicators are currently awaiting updated series data. Commercial shipping through the Strait of Hormuz remains near a complete standstill following conflict in Iran. According to IMF PortWatch data, single-day transits stood at 1.0 ship on November 1, 2026, keeping the 7-day transit average at 4.4 ships per day. Reopening resolution requires this 7-day moving average to climb back above 60 ships per day.

Forecast Rationale

The probability of a near-term reopening of the Strait remains very low. Crowd markets on Polymarket price the chance of traffic returning to normal by November 30 at just 2.4%. We project a 1.0% chance within one week and a 3.5% chance within one month, expanding to 18% over three months and 55% over a full year, reflecting that diplomatic resolution or naval de-mining will take considerable time once active hostilities subside.

Meanwhile, the forecast for a new conflict shock—defined specifically as a new war or blockade involving a top-five global exporter of oil, gas, fertilizer, or grain—remains close to long-term historical base rates. Because the current wars in Ukraine and Iran are already active and factored into baseline conditions, our 1-month probability for an additional major shock is 0.9%, rising to 11% at the 1-year horizon. While Crisis Group reporting highlights continued escalation risks in Iran, expanding the conflict to another top-tier exporter remains historically rare.

What to Watch

Key indicators to monitor include daily IMF PortWatch transit counts for early signs of commercial convoys returning, official maritime security alerts from regional naval task forces, and any signs of secondary disruptions in adjacent chokepoints like the Bab el-Mandeb or the Taiwan Strait.

What would change this view

  • A sustained rise in IMF PortWatch daily transits through the Strait of Hormuz above 20 to 30 ships/day would prompt an upward revision to near-term reopening odds.
  • Formal ceasefire talks or de-escalation agreements between Iran and regional adversaries that include maritime transit guarantees.
  • New military mobilization or naval blockades threatening major commodity transit hubs elsewhere, such as the Taiwan Strait.
