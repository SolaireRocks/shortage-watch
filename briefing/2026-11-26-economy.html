The overall economy index reads 29.6, remaining comfortably within the normal range. Real economic activity indicators show solid resilience. The real-time Sahm rule remains at 0.0 points, well below the 0.5-point threshold that signals the onset of a recession, reflecting stable employment conditions. In addition, physical activity proxies like weather- and weekday-adjusted electrical grid demand are positive at 1.05% above expected levels, and the 10-year minus 3-month yield curve is un-inverted at 0.51 points.

The primary sources of friction today are centered in financial markets rather than the real economy. The VIX index has climbed to 26.36, and high-yield credit spreads stand at 3.72 points, showing increased investor caution and risk repricing. However, broader monetary plumbing remains loose, with the Chicago Fed National Financial Conditions Index registering -0.444, well in negative territory.

Near-term recession risk remains low, pegged at 0.3% over the coming week and 2.8% over three months, closely tracking prediction market consensus of 2.6%. The 12-month outlook sits at 26%, slightly below market expectations (29%), supported by historical base rates and resilient domestic demand. No household financial defensive actions are warranted beyond normal budgeting.

  • Sustained credit market deterioration with high-yield spreads blowing out past 500 basis points alongside a tightening NFCI above zero.
