Current Assessment

The overall disease threat sits at elevated, driven primarily by a fast and early start to the seasonal influenza wave across the United States and in Ohio. The aggregate disease index reached 74.2, lifted by high stress scores in seasonal flu admissions, clinical test positivity, and strong El Niño climatic conditions.

Influenza hospitalizations nationally surged to 11,792 for the week of November 14—running 8,437 admissions above typical levels for this point in autumn. In Ohio, flu admissions climbed 126% over three weeks to 235, accompanied by a 9.4% clinical test positivity rate. Meanwhile, other common respiratory viruses remain comparatively restrained: COVID-19 hospital admissions (5,025 nationally; 160 in Ohio) and RSV admissions (1,626 nationally; 20 in Ohio) are tracking well below previous seasonal surges. All-cause respiratory emergency visits also remain slightly below historical multi-year baselines.

Pandemic Forecast Justification

We maintain our baseline pandemic emergence probabilities across all horizons (0.1% over 1 week, 0.4% over 1 month, 1.3% over 3 months, 2.6% over 6 months, and 5.5% over 1 year). The primary novel pathogen under observation remains avian influenza A(H5N1). A recent WHO Disease Outbreak News report confirmed three human infections and one death among Washington State agricultural workers. However, these events reflect direct poultry or dairy contact; no sustained person-to-person transmission has been documented.

Agricultural viral activity is evident, with wastewater H5 detections climbing to 2.16% and 3.75 million birds lost in the last 30 days. Because spillover without transmission efficiency does not satisfy the criteria for a pandemic emergency, historical base rates anchored around roughly one major respiratory pandemic emergence every two decades remain appropriate.

What to Watch

  1. Evidence of clustered H5N1 infections in household contacts or individuals without agricultural contact.
  2. Whether seasonal flu hospitalizations level off in December or accelerate into severe healthcare capacity strain.
  3. Trends in Ohio wastewater viral loads for influenza A and SARS-CoV-2 over the coming two weeks.

What would change this view

  • Confirmed cluster of secondary human-to-human transmission of H5N1 without direct animal exposure.
  • A formal WHO declaration of a Public Health Emergency of International Concern (PHEIC) for a novel respiratory pathogen.
  • Unexplained spikes in national all-cause respiratory ICU admissions or pneumonia deaths far above typical winter trajectories.

Novel pathogen indicators remain close to baseline expectations despite ongoing avian influenza activity. The WHO reported three confirmed human cases of Influenza A(H5N1) with one fatality among agricultural workers in Washington State, but these represent direct occupational spillovers rather than human-to-human transmission. Wastewater monitoring detected H5 in 2.16% of samples, up 96% over three weeks, alongside 3.75 million birds affected across 30 flocks in the past month. Broader indicators show no pandemic strain on the healthcare system: all-cause respiratory emergency visits stand at 13.34% (2.31 percentage points below typical seasonal norms), and pneumonia, flu, and COVID mortality remains subdued at 0.893 deaths per 100,000 weekly.

Other communicable diseases

Elevated

Seasonal influenza activity accelerates rapidly nationwide while COVID-19 and RSV remain moderate.

Seasonal influenza is driving the bulk of national disease activity with an unusually fast early-season surge. Hospital admissions for flu reached 11,792 for the week ending November 14, an 81% rise over three weeks and 8,437 admissions above the seasonal baseline. Emergency department visits attributed to flu rose to 2.48%, and clinical laboratory test positivity reached 11.755% (6.79 points above typical seasonal levels). Influenza A wastewater viral activity jumped 203% to 3.03. In contrast, COVID-19 hospital admissions stand at 5,025 (well below historical November marks), and RSV admissions remain modest at 1,626. Among notifiable conditions, indigenous measles (5,224 YTD vs. 1,613 last year) and cyclosporiasis continue running significantly above historical levels.

Ohio

Elevated

Ohio mirrors national flu acceleration with hospitalizations and positivity rising quickly.

In Ohio, respiratory illness is marked by a clear ramp in seasonal influenza. Flu hospital admissions jumped 126% over three weeks to 235 for the week ending November 14 (159.2 above typical seasonal levels), while clinical lab positivity reached 9.4% (+4.68 points above usual). Emergency visits for influenza rose to 1.47%. Overall acute respiratory emergency visits sit at 14.35%, slightly below the historical average (-0.86 points). COVID-19 activity remains contained with 160 weekly admissions and 23.64% of sewer sites testing at moderate or higher levels. RSV activity is minimal, accounting for just 0.04% of emergency visits and 20 hospitalizations. Notifiable conditions show elevated year-to-date counts for cyclosporiasis (6,754 vs. 73) and measles (218 vs. 32).
