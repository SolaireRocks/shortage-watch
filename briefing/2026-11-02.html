Overview and Household Risk Summary

The Scarcity Pressure Index (SPI) sits at 65.6, placing overall supply pressure in the elevated band. Today’s stress is heavily concentrated in refined petroleum products and crude oil, driven by the ongoing disruption in the Strait of Hormuz. Despite alarming headlines regarding international shipping and geopolitical tensions, US grocery shelves, natural gas distribution, and the broader consumer goods supply chain remain operational. Household risk remains primarily financial—facing steep prices at the pump and rising freight surcharges—rather than physical shortages of basic household necessities.

Energy and Critical Commodities

Hard energy metrics remain acutely stressed. Brent crude sits at $123.10/bbl (+87.9% year-over-year) and WTI at $103.37/bbl (+66.4% year-over-year), feeding directly into downstream fuel costs. National retail gasoline has risen to $4.269/gal (+40.7% YoY), while retail diesel has reached $6.352/gal (+70.8% YoY). In response to acute logistics strain, federal authorities have urged states to waive dyed diesel restrictions for agricultural users. Crowdsourced prediction markets place only a 6.5% probability on Hormuz transits normalizing before the end of November. While physical outages at retail stations remain localized rather than systemic, the fuel disruption threshold (a 25% pump price jump in 4 weeks or multi-state outages) carries an elevated 15% probability over 1 month and 28% over 3 months.

Natural gas (D = 0.04) and fertilizer (D = -0.12) show minimal domestic stress today, though retail UAN28 climbed 6% in October. In agriculture, global wheat remains elevated at $347/mt (+48.4% YoY), and Mexican feeder cattle restrictions add upstream cost pressures, but retail food stocks remain stable. Normal pantry maintenance (baseline) remains the appropriate posture.

Pharmaceuticals and Controlled Substances

The Pharmaceutical Scarcity Index stands at 35.8 (normal band). The ASHP reported 225 active shortages at the end of Q3 2026, well below historical peak crisis levels (323 in early 2024), while FDA active shortages count 69. This creates a significant jump in the 3-month risk (25%) unless regulators issue another extension.

What would change this view

  • Confirmed diplomatic resolution reopening the Strait of Hormuz with verified tanker traffic above 60 transits per day.
  • Widespread physical terminal outages or retail station run-outs spreading across three or more adjacent states.
  • Publication of an emergency extension or final DEA rule for Schedule II telemedicine prescribing before December 31.
