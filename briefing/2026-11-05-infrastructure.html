Current Infrastructure Posture

The infrastructure and natural hazards composite index sits at 66.3, holding in the elevated band following a 2.9-point increase over the past week. Elevated readings are driven largely by persistent digital threats rather than acute physical destruction. CISA has logged 34 actively exploited vulnerabilities over the trailing 30 days (a robust stress score of 1.69), including ongoing exploitation of network gateway zero-days such as Ivanti Connect Secure. While recent cyberattacks targeted commercial refrigeration systems in European supermarkets, domestic operational networks across top-tier utilities remain stable.

Physical indicators present a mixed but generally stable profile. The USGS recorded 8 earthquakes of magnitude 6.5 or higher globally over the last 30 days (stress score 1.69), yet these events have not struck critical domestic energy or transit corridors. The Global Disaster Alert and Coordination System (GDACS) lists only one active orange or red disaster alert worldwide (stress score -0.67). Space weather is also exceptionally calm, with NOAA SWPC reporting a daily maximum planetary Kp of 1.67, well below levels that would threaten bulk electric power transmission.

Forecast Justification

The baseline historical frequency for qualifying US infrastructure failures—such as the 2021 Colonial Pipeline shutdown, Winter Storm Uri, or the 2024 Change Healthcare outage—is approximately once every four to five years, implying a baseline 1-week probability of 0.43% and a 1-year probability of 20%. Because the pace of discovered and weaponized vulnerabilities remains high, near-term estimates remain elevated above baseline at 1.5% over 1 week and 5.0% over 1 month, stepping up to 26% over 1 year.

No specialized household protective action is recommended today beyond routine household preparedness, as primary fuel logistics, power generation, and pharmaceutical distribution networks are operating normally.

What would change this view

  • A confirmed cyber intrusion disabling operational technology at a major US pipeline, port authority, or transmission grid operator.
  • A severe geomagnetic storm (planetary Kp reaching 8 or 9) threatening electrical transformer equipment across North America.
  • A major domestic severe weather outbreak directly knocking out power or fuel delivery infrastructure for multi-day stretches.
