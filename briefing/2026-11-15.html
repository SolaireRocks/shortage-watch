Current Assessment: Elevated Strain Without Physical Shortage

The Shortage Pressure Index (SPI) sits at 61.8, remaining in the elevated band. The dominant driver continues to be the downstream energy complex. Refined fuel costs remain historically stiff, with retail diesel averaging $6.127 per gallon—up 59.7% year-over-year—and regular gasoline priced at $3.995 per gallon (+30.7% year-over-year). Freight operators and logistics providers report passing through fuel surcharges, including an 11% surcharge announced by the U.S. Postal Service, reinforcing broader price pressures throughout domestic distribution channels.

Despite high prices, hard data does not indicate widespread physical scarcity in the United States. Domestic commercial gasoline inventories stand at 210.76 million barrels, which is 0.6% above the five-year seasonal average. Crude benchmarks have softened marginally over the last month, with Brent at $92.14 per barrel and WTI at $89.62 per barrel. Oil domain hard data (D = 1.04) has cooled by 0.69 points over the last 30 days. Although the Strategic Petroleum Reserve remains low at 279.5 million barrels (-31.9% year-over-year), immediate retail fuel availability remains stable.

Other Domains

  • Food: Global agricultural grain benchmarks remain firm, with World Bank wheat at $317 per metric ton (+37.3% YoY) and rice at $489 per metric ton (+37.4% YoY). Domestic grocery CPI increased 0.5% in October, led by meats and fresh produce. However, national retail supply chains remain well stocked, with contiguous US drought area falling 6.4 points below its five-year average (41.68%) and zero firsthand grocery hoarding signals reported.
  • Natural Gas & Heating: The natural gas domain hard data index is neutral at -0.03, showing that domestic storage and production are adequately balanced for early winter demand.
  • Fertilizer: Retail fertilizer prices showed mixed movements in early November, with the domain index at 0.01. Input costs remain manageable for upcoming spring prep.

What to Watch

We monitor whether high diesel crack spreads persist through the peak holiday shipping season and whether Strait of Hormuz commercial traffic (currently averaging 3.9 ships per day) experiences renewed geopolitical interruption. For a typical US household, maintain baseline emergency pantry provisions (two weeks of shelf-stable food and water) and keep vehicle tanks reasonably filled, but avoid reactive or panic buying.

What would change this view

  • A rapid regional drawdown in US motor fuel inventories combined with unplanned refinery outages that drives pump rationing.
  • A sharp escalation in Persian Gulf maritime conflict halting commercial transit or damaging energy loading terminals.
  • Early severe winter weather driving Henry Hub natural gas cash prices to more than twice their 1-year median.
