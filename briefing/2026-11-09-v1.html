Current Scarcity Assessment

The Shortage Pressure Index (SPI) stands at 65.1, remaining in the elevated band. While physical scarcity at the consumer level remains virtually non-existent across US grocery shelves and utility connections, upstream cost pressures in petroleum and freight continue to exert persistent stress.

Fuel and Transportation

The oil and fuels domain continues to represent the primary pocket of stress, registering a hard data index of 1.51. US regular retail gasoline is priced at $4.153 per gallon (+37.6% year-over-year), while retail diesel remains heavily stressed at $6.292 per gallon (+67.7% year-over-year). Elevated crude prices—Brent at $107.03/bbl and WTI at $99.64/bbl—coupled with a depleted Strategic Petroleum Reserve (down 31.1% year-over-year to 282.2 million barrels) leave domestic refining and distribution networks with thin margins for error.

Upstream shipping chokepoints remain severely constrained: Strait of Hormuz transits average only 4.4 commercial ships per day. Domestically, truckload rates have risen and freight capacity has contracted for 11 consecutive months. However, wholesale diesel futures have shown mild easing, and localized pump outages have not materialized, holding near-term qualifying physical shortage odds at modest levels.

Food, Natural Gas, and Fertilizer

  • Food (Hard Data: 0.72): International crop markets reflect significant inflation, with global wheat up 37.3% and rice up 37.4% year-over-year. Nevertheless, US domestic production conditions remain stable, with contiguous US drought covering 43.98% of land area—4.6 percentage points below the 5-year average. Grocery shelves remain well-stocked, and firsthand consumer hoarding indicators are absent.
  • Natural Gas (Hard Data: -0.02): Gas supplies entering the winter heating season are well within historical parameters. Storage levels and Henry Hub pricing show no acute stress, keeping near-term residential curtailment risk at baseline base-rate levels.
  • Fertilizer (Hard Data: 0.00): While seven of eight major fertilizer products posted modest price gains in late October (notably UAN28), application availability across agricultural retail remains orderly.

Household Guidance

Maintain baseline emergency pantry supplies (several days to two weeks of shelf-stable food and water). Because high diesel prices and freight constraints create lingering pump price volatility, households should consider keeping vehicle tanks at least half-full as a low-cost protective buffer.

What would change this view

  • Strait of Hormuz commercial traffic rebounding above 30 ships per day would significantly lower fuel price trajectories and refinery margin pressure.
  • A sudden physical outage or pipeline rupture shutting down refined product distribution to the East Coast or Southeast for more than 48 hours would push 1-week and 1-month fuel disruption probabilities sharply higher.
  • Widespread regional grocery purchase rationing or a single-month surge in CPI Food at Home exceeding 2.0% would elevate food scarcity assessments.

What to do

  • Prepare Fuel (gasoline, diesel)
    Keep personal vehicle fuel tanks at or above half-full to insulate against local price volatility and regional spot delivery delays caused by tight diesel inventories.
