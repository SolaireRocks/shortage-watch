Choke Point and Energy Corridor Tracking

Maritime traffic through the Strait of Hormuz remains effectively halted. According to IMF PortWatch data from November 15, daily transits registered at 2.0 vessels, bringing the 7-day moving average to 2.3 vessels per day. Reopening requires the 7-day average to cross 60 transits per day. While crowd pricing on Polymarket for a 1-month reopening is priced at 50.0%, this appears distorted given that the 3-month market trades at only 8.5% and physical shipping flows show no signs of immediate normalization. We hold the 1-month reopening probability at 2.5%, climbing to 10.0% over 3 months and 60.0% by 1 year.

Global Conflict and Infrastructure

Broader geopolitical stress remains steady. Global conflict fatalities recorded by the Uppsala Conflict Data Program (UCDP) logged at 13,286 for September, yielding a slightly negative stress score (-0.07). Satellite monitoring via NASA FIRMS recorded zero listed energy or fertilizer infrastructure sites with abnormal thermal detections, reflecting stable baseline operations outside active combat zones. Recent reports from the International Crisis Group highlight deepening humanitarian instability and renewed violence in Sudan and South Sudan, but these conflicts do not meet the operational threshold for a global commodity supply shock involving top-five exporters.

Household Outlook

Because baseline risk metrics remain steady and the Hormuz disruption is already priced into commercial routing, no immediate protective actions are necessary today beyond maintaining standard household reserves. We continue to monitor PortWatch AIS vessel counts and thermal anomaly alerts for changes in international transit lanes.

What would change this view

  • New military interdictions or blockades directly involving top-5 agricultural or energy producers (such as Russia, Saudi Arabia, or the Taiwan Strait) would significantly lift conflict_shock estimates.
