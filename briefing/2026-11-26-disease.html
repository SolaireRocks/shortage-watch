The disease threat environment is currently elevated, driven primarily by an unseasonably steep surge in seasonal influenza alongside continued avian influenza developments.

Nationally, seasonal influenza metrics have accelerated well ahead of typical late-autumn timelines. Hospital admissions reached 11,792 for the week ending November 14, 2026, exceeding normal levels for this week by 8,437 admissions and marking an 81.0% climb over three weeks. Emergency department visits for influenza stand at 3.29%, while clinical test positivity has reached 11.755%. In Ohio, the trajectory is equally sharp: influenza admissions reached 235 (up 126.0% over three weeks, 159.2 above typical baselines) and clinical positivity reached 9.4%. Conversely, COVID-19 hospitalizations remain far below previous years (5,025 nationally and 160 in Ohio), and RSV clinical indicators remain low.

On the novel pathogen front, the WHO issued a Disease Outbreak News report on November 24 confirming three human H5N1 cases among agricultural workers in Washington State, with one death. Concurrently, US flock losses totaled 4.02 million birds over the past 30 days (+47.0% over three weeks), and H5 detections in wastewater samples rose to 2.16%. However, these cases reflect direct animal-to-human spillover. Because there is no evidence of sustained human-to-human transmission, pandemic probabilities remain anchored near historical base rates, sitting at 0.1% over one week, 0.4% over one month, and 5.5% over one year.

Households should focus on practical seasonal measures: verify that seasonal flu immunizations are current, keep home stocks of rapid tests and fever reducers on hand, and monitor public health updates for any changes in H5N1 transmission patterns.

What would change this view

  • Confirmation of secondary or sustained person-to-person transmission of H5N1 without direct animal exposure.
  • A formal declaration by WHO of a Public Health Emergency of International Concern (PHEIC) for a novel respiratory agent.

Ohio

Ohio mirrors national trends with a steep rise in influenza admissions and elevated measles counts.

In Ohio, respiratory illness is dominated by a fast-moving influenza wave. Weekly hospital admissions for influenza rose 126.0% over three weeks to 235 for the week ending November 14, running 159.2 admissions above seasonal norms (stress score 2.86). Clinical laboratory test positivity for flu reached 9.4% (up 123.0% over three weeks and 4.68 percentage points above usual), while influenza emergency visits rose to 2.15%. Total acute respiratory emergency visits (15.18%) and doctor visits for flu-like illness (2.354%) remain below typical historical levels for late November. COVID-19 hospitalizations in Ohio totaled 160 admissions, well below historical averages (-810.4). On the notifiable diseases ledger, Ohio reports 238 indigenous measles cases year-to-date (including 19 this week) versus 32 at this point last year, alongside 6,756 cyclosporiasis cases compared to 73 last year.
