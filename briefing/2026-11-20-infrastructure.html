Cyber and Supply Chain Drivers

Elevated stress persists primarily in cybersecurity. The Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA) has tracked 32 newly exploited vulnerabilities in the last 30 days, alongside one actively exploited vulnerability in industrial control systems over the past 90 days. Recent law enforcement actions reported by The Record and BleepingComputer highlight ongoing ransomware pressure against energy and healthcare targets, including extraditions and arrests tied to campaigns that historically sought to extort US oil and hospital networks. While these arrests demonstrate defensive momentum, the underlying attack surface across critical operators remains substantial.

Assessment and Outlook

We assess the probability of a major three-day infrastructure event (such as a pipeline shutdown, top-10 port closure, regional grid collapse, or claims clearinghouse failure) at 1.5% over the coming week, 5% over one month, and 26% over the full year. These estimates sit slightly above long-run historical base rates—which average roughly one major incident every four to five years—to account for the continuous volume of exploited CVEs and persistent targeting of industrial assets. However, in the absence of acute winter storms or imminent physical shocks, short-term household risk remains baseline.

What We Are Watching

We monitor daily space weather readings for sudden coronal mass ejections, CISA advisories on zero-day vulnerabilities affecting operational technology, and early seasonal winter weather advisories that could strain natural gas and electrical transmission.

What would change this view

  • A severe geomagnetic storm (Kp 8 or higher) threatening high-voltage electrical transformers.
  • Severe winter weather or physical damage causing extended, multi-state utility outages.
