Current Assessment

The broad Economy index stands at 28.0, firmly inside the normal range (under 60). While the index rose 6.8 points over the past week, that movement was primarily driven by financial market volatility rather than deterioration in the real economy. Equity market turbulence pushed the VIX volatility index to 25.46, and high-yield credit spreads ticked up to 3.43 points. However, broader financial conditions as measured by the Chicago Fed remain accommodative at -0.478.

Underlying economic activity and labour market fundamentals show notable strength. Initial jobless claims for the week ending November 14 came in at 203,000, consistent with robust employment retention. Crucially for our recession tracking, the real-time Sahm rule reading is 0.0 points, well below the 0.5-point trigger that signals a recessionary downturn. Physical activity metrics also confirm steady output, with weather- and weekday-adjusted grid electricity demand running 0.67% above expected levels over the past four weeks.

Forecast and What to Watch

Near-term recession risk remains very low, estimated at 0.3% over one week, 1.3% over one month, and 3.0% over three months. Prediction markets show a similarly modest 1.9% implied probability over three months. Looking out to the 12-month horizon, recession odds sit at 25%, reflecting baseline cyclical risks and aligning closely with prediction market consensus at 26%.

For households, conditions require no extraordinary defensive financial posture beyond standard household budgeting and maintaining regular emergency savings. Key indicators to watch in the coming weeks include whether higher market volatility bleeds into sustained credit spread widening, or whether weekly jobless claims rise significantly from their current low base.

What would change this view

  • Initial jobless claims rising sustainedly above 260,000 or the Sahm rule indicator rising toward the 0.5 threshold.
  • High-yield credit spreads widening rapidly past 5.0 points alongside tightening Chicago Fed financial conditions.
