Assessment of Probabilities

The baseline probability of a true pandemic emergency—defined as a WHO PHEIC declaration for a new pathogen or confirmed sustained human-to-human H5N1 transmission within the United States—is historically rare, averaging roughly once every twenty years (~5% per year). Current respiratory trends reflect expected seasonal patterns for established pathogens (influenza and endemic COVID-19) rather than the emergence of a novel agent. Because there is no evidence of novel pathogen spread or mammalian adaptation enabling human-to-human transmission of avian influenza in current reporting, forward-looking probabilities remain close to baseline: 0.1% over 1 week, 0.4% over 1 month, 1.3% over 3 months, 2.6% over 6 months, and 5.5% over 1 year.

What to Watch

Over the coming weeks, attention should focus on CDC respiratory surveillance to track whether influenza and COVID-19 hospitalizations accelerate past historical seasonal thresholds. In addition, ongoing genomic surveillance of avian influenza in US dairy herds and poultry operations remains critical to detect any mutations associated with efficient mammalian airborne transmission.
