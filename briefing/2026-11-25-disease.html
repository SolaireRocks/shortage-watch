The disease risk index registers at an elevated 74.9, primarily driven by a steep and early acceleration of seasonal influenza rather than novel pandemic pathogen emergence. Nationwide, weekly influenza hospital admissions reached 11,792 for the week ending November 14, which is 8,437 admissions above typical seasonal averages and represents an 81% increase over three weeks. Emergency department visits for influenza have similarly expanded to 3.29% of all visits, with clinical positivity at 11.755%. In Ohio, the pattern matches the national trajectory: influenza test positivity stands at 9.40% and weekly flu hospitalizations rose 126% over three weeks to 235 admissions.

On the pandemic horizon, the WHO confirmed three human cases of H5N1 avian influenza among agricultural workers in Washington State, including one death. In addition, wastewater detections of the H5 marker rose to 2.16% of sites, and 4.02 million commercial birds were lost across 32 infected flocks over the past 30 days. Despite these alarming headlines, these infections reflect occupational exposure rather than secondary or community spread. Because the definition of a pandemic event requires sustained human-to-human transmission in the United States or a WHO Public Health Emergency of International Concern for a new pathogen, probabilities remain anchored close to historical base rates: 0.1% over one week, 0.4% over one month, 1.3% over three months, 2.6% over six months, and 5.5% over one year.

Other common viral pathogens are subdued: COVID-19 hospital admissions nationwide (5,025/week) are running more than 14,000 admissions below the historical multi-year average for mid-November, and RSV activity remains quiet. Households should observe routine seasonal precautions, ensuring family members have received updated seasonal flu shots and maintaining home fever-reducing medications as the seasonal flu wave continues its early climb.

Pandemic-level threat

Elevated

Spillover avian influenza cases reported in Washington State farmworkers without sustained transmission.

Novel influenza surveillance shows localized agricultural transmission of H5N1. The World Health Organization confirmed three human H5N1 infections among agricultural workers in Washington State, resulting in one fatality. Wastewater detection of H5 genetic material rose 96% over three weeks to 2.16% of monitored sites, while commercial flocks lost 4.02 million birds over the past 30 days across 32 confirmed operations. Crucially, all documented infections involve direct occupational contact with affected poultry or livestock, with zero evidence of community spread or sustained human-to-human transmission. Broader US respiratory burden remains controlled: emergency visits for all acute respiratory illness stand at 14.48%, which is 1.89 percentage points below historical late-autumn benchmarks, and pneumonia, flu, or COVID deaths are tracking at 0.893 per 100,000 per week. Base pandemic probabilities remain firmly anchored near historical baseline levels.

Emergency department visits attributed to flu rose to 3.29% (+1.04 points vs. typical weeks), and clinical lab test positivity jumped to 11.755% (+6.79 points above baseline). Conversely, COVID-19 indicators remain suppressed, with emergency visits at 0.71% and hospital admissions at 5,025 (14,551 below seasonal norms). RSV admissions stand at 1,626 per week, close to seasonal norms. Among notifiable conditions, national cyclosporiasis (33,567 cases YTD) and indigenous measles (5,224 cases YTD) continue to trend substantially above 2025 numbers.

Ohio mirrors national flu acceleration while overall respiratory hospitalizations remain manageable.

In Ohio, respiratory illness trends reflect a rapid onset of the seasonal flu wave. Emergency department visits for influenza reached 2.15% (+126% over three weeks), clinical lab positivity rose to 9.40% (4.68 points above seasonal norms), and weekly flu admissions reached 235 (up 159.2 admissions over the 3-to-5 year baseline). However, aggregate acute respiratory emergency visits sit at 15.18%, slightly below the historical average (-0.98 points), and pneumonia, flu, or COVID mortality remains low at 1.059 per 100,000. COVID-19 hospital admissions in Ohio are mild at 160 per week, and RSV emergency visits are minimal at 0.07%. In state notifiable disease monitoring, indigenous measles (218 cases YTD vs. 32 in 2025) and cyclosporiasis (6,754 vs. 73) remain significantly elevated compared to the same period last year.
