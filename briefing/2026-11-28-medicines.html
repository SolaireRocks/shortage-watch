Current Supply Conditions

Overall prescription drug availability across the United States remains stable. The Pharmaceutical Scarcity Index (PSI) registers at 31.0, reflecting a normal operating band. According to the American Society of Health-System Pharmacists (ASHP), active shortages stood at 225 at the end of the third quarter of 2026, well below the peak of 323 observed in early 2024. While clinical hurdles persist in hospital settings for select oncology agents and injectable contrast media, oral generic retail drugs show minimal price volatility, with generic price-shock breadth measuring only 0.6%.

Near-Term Risks and Key Dates

The most acute disruption risk centers on regulatory access rather than factory output. The temporary federal flexibilities that allow clinicians to prescribe Schedule II controlled substances via telemedicine without an initial in-person visit are scheduled to expire on December 31, 2026—just 33 days away. Although the DEA and HHS have issued temporary extensions four times previously, no permanent special registration rule has been finalized. Consequently, the three-month probability of a qualifying event remains elevated at 25% to account for potential administrative disruption if an extension or final rule is delayed past year-end.

What would change this view

  • A DEA interim final rule or further temporary extension continuing Schedule II telemedicine prescribing flexibilities into 2027.
  • A sharp surge in generic oral antibiotic or respiratory drug shortages driven by winter viral illness pressure.
