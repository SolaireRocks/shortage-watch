Current Shortage Risk Overview

The Shortage Pressure Index (SPI) stands at 64.5, placing overall national supply risk in the elevated band. Today's primary driver continues to be liquid fuels and transportation logistics, while domestic agricultural, natural gas, and fertilizer channels show resilient physical supply.

Energy and Transportation Pressures

The fuel sector exhibits substantial hard-data stress ($D = 1.06$), driven by the ongoing maritime bottleneck in the Middle East. With Strait of Hormuz transits stalled at an average of 2.3 vessels per day (well below normal traffic of 60 per day), crude benchmarks remain firm with Brent at $92.14/bbl (+44.3% year-over-year) and WTI at $89.62/bbl (+47.1% year-over-year). In the United States, retail regular gasoline averages $3.805 per gallon (+24.3% YoY), and on-highway diesel stands at $5.937 per gallon (+53.5% YoY).

Despite these elevated price levels, physical availability remains stable. Commercial US gasoline inventories sit at 210.8 million barrels, which is 0.6% above the five-year seasonal average. However, strategic cushions are narrower than historical norms, with the Strategic Petroleum Reserve down 31.9% year-over-year at 279.5 million barrels. Sustained diesel expenses are actively propagating through supply chains, contributing to freight rate hikes and parcel surcharges.

Food, Fertilizer, and Natural Gas

  • Food ($D = 0.73$): Upstream commodity benchmarks remain elevated, including world wheat at $317.0/mt (+37.3% YoY) and world rice at $489.0/mt (+37.4% YoY). Coupled with diesel and logistics costs, grocery store inflation is expected to see moderate upward pressure, particularly in fresh produce and meat. Nonetheless, domestic growing conditions are favorable, with contiguous US drought coverage at 41.68% (6.4 points below the five-year average), and national grocery supply lines remain unconstrained.
  • Natural Gas ($D = -0.06$): Hard data indicators are balanced, and domestic storage is well prepared for early winter demand. Utility curtailment risks remain near historical baseline rates.
  • Fertilizer ($D = 0.01$): While retail quotes for five of eight major fertilizers ticked slightly upward in mid-November, wholesale distribution networks report stable throughput ahead of spring application season.

Household Guidance

Household shortage conditions do not warrant emergency pantry expansions. Maintain a standard baseline supply of non-perishable staples (several days to two weeks). Because elevated diesel prices and freight friction can create localized delivery volatility, keeping personal vehicle fuel tanks at or above half full is a sensible, low-cost precaution.

What would change this view

  • A sustained reopening of the Strait of Hormuz with commercial transits returning above 60 vessels per day, which would significantly reduce crude and distillate crack spreads.
  • A rapid drawdown in US domestic gasoline and distillate inventories falling more than 10% below 5-year averages, which would heighten near-term fuel outage probabilities.
  • Early winter Arctic outbreaks triggering wellhead freeze-offs and Henry Hub natural gas spikes exceeding double the 1-year median.
