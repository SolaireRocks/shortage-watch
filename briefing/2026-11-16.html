Current Shortage Risk Assessment

The Shortage Pressure Index (SPI) sits at 64.7, remaining in the elevated band. Today's environment is characterized by sharp price pressures in transport and refined fuels alongside steady physical availability in consumer grocery and utility channels.

Fuel and Logistics

Fuel markets remain the principal source of supply-chain friction. Retail diesel is holding above $6.12 per gallon—59.7% above year-ago levels—while regular gasoline sits near $4.00 per gallon. Geopolitical conflict in the Middle East continues to restrict Strait of Hormuz tanker movements to roughly 3.9 vessels per day compared to the historical norm of 60 per day. While this disruption has driven up shipping spot rates and bunker surcharges, domestic physical supply remains intact: US gasoline inventories are slightly above their 5-year seasonal average at 210.8 million barrels. Because physical stockouts remain localized to logistics margins rather than retail distribution systems, the near-term probability of a qualifying regional fuel shortage remains modest at 4% over the next week and 15% over the next month, though elevated over the 6- to 12-month window.

Food and Agriculture

Food supply indicators show significant divergence between global market prices and domestic shelf availability. Global benchmark prices for wheat ($317/mt) and rice ($489/mt) are approximately 37% higher year-over-year, and domestic consumer food prices rose 0.5% in October. However, contiguous US drought coverage has moderated to 41.68% (6.4 points below the seasonal 5-year average), and consumer hoarding signals are completely absent (z = 0.0). The probability of widespread staple limits or empty shelves remains low at 1.5% over the next month.

Natural Gas and Fertilizer

Natural gas and fertilizer domains remain quiet. Domestic natural gas indicators show virtually zero hard-data stress (D = -0.04), and retail fertilizer prices remain stable without dealer-level application bottlenecks. Both domains carry low short-term shortage probabilities of well under 2% over the next month.

Household Guidance

No extraordinary consumer actions or preemptive buying runs are recommended. Households should maintain a baseline emergency pantry containing several days to two weeks of non-perishable food, water, and essential household supplies. Fuel costs remain high, but physical availability at retail stations remains dependable.

What would change this view

  • Strait of Hormuz daily commercial transits recovering toward the 60-ship baseline, which would rapidly deflate global crude and freight risk premiums.
  • A sudden drop in US refined product inventories or unexpected refinery shutdowns that trigger regional rack rationing or dry fuel pumps.
  • Consumer shelf-clearing or widespread retail staple purchase limits appearing across multiple national grocery banners.
