Current Situation

The Shortage Pressure Index (SPI) sits at 56.7 on November 26, 2026, remaining well within the normal band (below 60.0) and down 2.6 points over the past week. Domestic supply chains are largely functioning as expected, and retail shelves remain stably supplied. Hoarding activity is absent, with the firsthand hoarding signal reading 0.0.

Upstream energy markets, however, continue to register significant cost stress. Brent crude is trading at $91.80 per barrel (up 41.6% year-over-year) and WTI crude sits at $82.24 per barrel (+39.1% YoY). This sustained oil price level is mirrored at the pump, particularly for commercial transport: US retail diesel is $5.695 per gallon, up 48.7% compared to last year. The primary driver of energy market tension remains the Middle East transit bottleneck, with IMF PortWatch recording Strait of Hormuz transits averaging just 2.7 vessels per day—far below the baseline 60 ships per day. Despite these global headwinds, domestic physical availability remains intact: US commercial gasoline inventories are 2.6% above their five-year seasonal average at 215.9 million barrels, buffering drivers against localized run-outs.

Food markets are showing secondary pressure rather than direct physical deficits. World market prices for staples are elevated—US HRW wheat is up 37.3% YoY to $317/mt and Thai rice is up 37.4% YoY to $489/mt. Domestically, FreightWaves reports that refrigerated truckload (reefer) tender rejections have doubled compared to last year, introducing friction into holiday grocery logistics. Nevertheless, earnings reports from major retail grocers such as Walmart continue to indicate healthy inventory flow and steady consumer supplies.

Why These Probabilities?

Qualifying US shortages are historically rare. A severe retail fuel event (multi-state station dry-outs for over 3 days or pump price surges of 25% in 4 weeks) has a historical base rate of about 1.3% per month. Because Strait of Hormuz transits remain severely constrained and distillate inventories are 6.8% below five-year averages, fuel risk is elevated well above base rates to 4% over 1 week, 15% over 1 month, and 28% over 3 months. However, robust domestic gasoline stocks prevent near-term probabilities from rising further.

For food, natural gas, and fertilizer, conditions do not warrant elevated warnings. Food 1-month risk is assessed at 1.5% and 3-month risk at 4.0%, as elevated commodity prices and reefer rejection rates translate to localized price inflation rather than widespread empty shelves. Natural gas and fertilizer hard data indices are near neutral (-0.05 and 0.01, respectively).

What to Watch

Key indicators to monitor in the coming weeks include: - Strait of Hormuz transits: An increase toward the 60 ships/day mark would alleviate long-term fuel risks, whereas prolonged stagnation keeps diesel and heating oil under pressure. - Holiday refrigerated freight capacity: Sustained high rejection rates could drive spot produce price spikes. - Winter weather patterns: Early-season severe freezes that could test regional natural gas pipeline deliveries.

What would change this view

  • A recovery of Strait of Hormuz traffic toward the historical baseline of 60 transits per day, which would significantly reduce 3-month to 1-year fuel shortage odds.
  • A prolonged shutdown or strike across West Coast ports or rail lines that halts agricultural distribution networks.
  • A major arctic blast early in the heating season pushing natural gas spot prices above twice their one-year median.
