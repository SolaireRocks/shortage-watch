The overall disease risk level stands at elevated, driven primarily by an unusually rapid start to the seasonal influenza wave across the United States, rather than a pandemic-scale novel pathogen emergency. Hospital admissions for influenza nationally reached 11,792 admissions for the week of November 14, exceeding the multi-year seasonal benchmark by 8,437 admissions (a robust stress score of 4.0). Overall respiratory emergency visits (14.48%) and pneumonia, flu, or COVID mortality (0.963 per 100,000) remain at or below expected levels for this point in November, confirming that respiratory pressure is confined to standard seasonal pathogens rather than an uncontained novel outbreak. We maintain the 1-year pandemic probability at 0.055, consistent with historical emergence rates.

What would change this view

  • Genetic sequencing demonstrating human-adaptive mutations in mammalian H5N1 isolates.
  • Confirmed secondary human-to-human transmission of avian influenza without direct animal contact.
  • A sudden divergence in unclassified acute respiratory hospital admissions outstripping seasonal flu testing capacity.
