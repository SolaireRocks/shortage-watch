Current State

The aggregate Economy index stands at 34.8 out of 100, remaining well inside the normal range despite recent financial market turbulence. Market stress flared sharply in late November, with the VIX equity volatility index rising to 35.89 points (a stress score of 4.0) and high-yield credit spreads widening to 3.76 points. However, broader financial measures indicate that credit pipes remain functional: the Chicago Fed National Financial Conditions Index was -0.478 as of mid-November, reflecting relatively loose overall conditions. Furthermore, the 10-year minus 3-month yield spread remains positive at 0.55 points, and weather-adjusted power demand is steady at 0.976 of expected load.

Why These Probabilities

The baseline probability of entering a recession over very short horizons is constrained by hard employment and activity data. The real-time Sahm rule indicator reads 0.0 points, far below the 0.50-point threshold that traditionally signals the start of a downturn. While stock market drawdowns often generate headlines, sharp volatility spikes do not reliably translate into immediate real economic contraction unless paired with sustained credit freezing and rapid labor deterioration. We keep the near-term risk low at 0.3% over 1 week, 1.3% over 1 month, and 2.8% over 3 months. Over the 1-year horizon, probability is assessed at 26%, in line with prediction market benchmarks (Kalshi at 21.5% and Polymarket at 31.5%) and above the historical annual baseline of 15% due to heightened macro uncertainty.

What to Watch

Key signals over the coming weeks include whether high-yield spreads widen past 500 basis points and whether the Chicago Fed index crosses into positive territory, indicating systemic tightening. Crucially, any upward drift in unemployment that moves the Sahm rule off zero would warrant an upward revision to near-term recession odds.

What would change this view

  • A sustained rise in the real-time Sahm rule toward the 0.5 trigger threshold.
  • Continuation of credit market widening pushing Chicago Fed financial conditions firmly into positive restrictive territory.
  • Significant contraction in high-frequency production indicators such as weather-adjusted electricity grid demand.
