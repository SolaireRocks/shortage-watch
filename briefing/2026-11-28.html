Current Situation

The Shortage Pressure Index (SPI) sits at 50.1, placing aggregate supply conditions squarely in the normal band. While global energy prices and international commodity benchmarks remain elevated compared to historical averages, domestic physical supply chains in the United States continue to function smoothly. Wholesale inventories and transportation networks are absorbing holiday seasonal volume without signs of severe breakdown.

Domain Analysis

  • Fuel and Energy: Refined fuel prices represent the primary pocket of friction. National retail diesel sits at $5.695 per gallon—a 48.7% increase year-over-year—and crude benchmarks remain high, with Brent at $91.80 per barrel and WTI at $82.24 per barrel. In addition, Strait of Hormuz maritime transits remain depressed at 2.7 ships per day. However, US downstream infrastructure remains adequately supplied: domestic gasoline inventories are currently 2.6% above their five-year seasonal average, and retail diesel price declines have begun picking up pace according to recent logistics reporting. The probability of an acute multi-state physical outage within the next 30 days is estimated at 15%.
  • Food: Global agricultural commodity prices show sustained price elevation, with international wheat up 37.3% and rice up 37.4% year-over-year. Domestically, freight markets report that refrigerated truckload tender rejections have doubled compared to last year, creating shipping tightness around perishable holiday staples. Nevertheless, grocery shelf continuity remains intact, upstream disease concerns such as H5N1 have produced no sustained human-to-human spread, and consumer panic buying indicators remain flat (hoarding signal z-score of 0.0).
  • Natural Gas & Fertilizer: Both categories exhibit near-zero hard-data stress scores. Natural gas storage levels have stabilized heading into winter, and retail fertilizer markets are balanced, with modest price changes distributed across nutrients without supply shortages.

Household Strategy and What to Watch

Because physical supplies are intact and the 3-month food disruption probability is low (4%), households should maintain standard baseline preparedness. A routine pantry with one to two weeks of shelf-stable groceries, basic water storage, and customary vehicle fueling habits is sufficient. We will monitor winter weather patterns for early cold snaps that could test regional natural gas delivery, as well as freight capacity indicators following the holiday peak.

What would change this view

  • A multi-state physical fuel distribution disruption caused by refinery outages or pipeline failures that rapidly depletes regional terminal inventories.
  • A sharp spike in household panic buying or shelf clearance that pushes the hoarding signal z-score above 2.0.
  • Evidence of sustained human-to-human transmission of avian influenza causing severe processing plant shutdowns or widespread supply chain labor absenteeism.
