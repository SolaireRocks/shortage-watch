Current Situation

The Shortage Pressure Index (SPI) sits at 64.9, remaining in the elevated band. Today's primary strain continues to be concentrated in energy and downstream transport. Crude oil prices remain steep—Brent is trading at $92.94/bbl (+47.2% YoY) and WTI at $89.53/bbl (+47.6% YoY)—which has driven national retail diesel to $5.937 per gallon. This fuel cost inflation is rippling through logistics, where spot freight rates are up more than 50% year-over-year and carriers are reporting elevated tender rejections.

Geopolitical chokepoints remain severely constrained: the Strait of Hormuz is recording just 2.3 transits per day according to IMF PortWatch data, compared to the normal recovery threshold of 60 transits per day. In contrast, domestic physical petroleum stocks offer some cushion. Commercial gasoline inventories are running 2.6% above their five-year seasonal average, although distillate inventories are 6.8% below seasonal averages and the Strategic Petroleum Reserve is 32.7% lower year-over-year at 276.4 million barrels.

Food and fertilizer markets show price friction rather than acute physical shortages. Global crop staples remain expensive, with international wheat up 37.3% and rice up 37.4% year-over-year. While ex-military and defense warnings have highlighted potential vulnerabilities in sulfur and fertilizer import chains, domestic retail supplies of agricultural inputs and grocery staples are moving through distribution channels without widespread outages.

Why These Probabilities?

Historical base rates for widespread US shortages are inherently low: multi-state fuel outages occur roughly once every 6 to 8 years (typically around Gulf hurricane landfalls or pipeline cyberattacks), while qualifying food disruptions occur roughly once in 20 years. Because domestic refinery throughput and gasoline stocks are currently intact, the near-term risk of physical fuel starvation remains contained (1-week at 4%, 1-month at 15%). However, because prolonged chokepoints at Hormuz create structural upside risk for global crude, the 3-month (28%) and 1-year (45%) fuel disruption odds remain elevated above base rates.

Food shortage probabilities remain pegged at historical baselines (1.5% at 1 month, 4% at 3 months). The food fear gap (-0.17) is near zero, and consumer hoarding is nonexistent (hoarding signal z = 0.0), indicating ordinary shelf replenishment across all major grocery categories.

What to Watch

  • Strait of Hormuz Traffic: Any sustained return toward historical transit volumes would rapidly relieve global crude premiums.
  • Distillate Stock Draws: Weekly EIA inventory reports will indicate whether winter diesel and heating oil supplies continue to deplete below the current -6.8% deficit.
  • Freight Spot Volatility: Continued escalation in trucking tender rejections could trigger selective regional delivery lags heading into peak holiday shipping.

What would change this view

  • A recovery in Strait of Hormuz commercial traffic above 30 ships per day, which would significantly reduce the medium-term probability of refined product shocks.
  • A multi-state retail fuel outage lasting longer than 72 hours following an East Coast refinery issue or pipeline failure.
  • Sharp double-digit spikes in North American wholesale ammonia or natural gas spot prices heading into winter heating demand.

What to do

  • Prepare Fuel (gasoline, diesel)
    Keep personal vehicle tanks at or above half full. Elevated diesel prices ($5.94/gal) and ongoing Hormuz transit bottlenecks leave regional distribution systems vulnerable to unexpected freight or weather disruptions.
