Current Shortage Risk Overview

The Shortage Pressure Index (SPI) sits at 62.3, remaining in the elevated band. While input cost pressures in the energy sector remain substantial, physical availability across consumer staples in the United States continues to hold steady. Base rates indicate that severe retail shortages are rare outside of acute natural catastrophes or severe distribution failures; today's indicators do not point to near-term breakdown.

Primary Domain Pressures

  • Fuel & Energy: Petroleum markets represent the clearest area of sustained tension. Brent crude sits at $92.94 per barrel (+47.2% year-over-year) and WTI crude trades at $89.53 per barrel (+47.6% year-over-year). Strait of Hormuz tanker transits remain severely depressed at an average of 2.7 ships per day, far below the standard 60-ship baseline. Domestically, national retail diesel is high at $5.937 per gallon (+53.5% YoY), while regular gasoline stands at $3.805 per gallon (+24.3% YoY). Despite these elevated pump prices, physical fuel shortages are averted because US gasoline inventories stand at 215.9 million barrels (2.6% above the 5-year average), providing an adequate operational cushion.
  • Food & Groceries: International agricultural benchmarks remain elevated, with World Bank wheat at $317.00/mt (+37.3% YoY) and Thai rice at $489.00/mt (+37.4% YoY). Global acute food insecurity remains critical abroad (159.85 million people in IPC 3+), and domestic Thanksgiving costs rose 6.3% year-over-year. However, domestic retail food distribution channels remain fully functional. The food fear gap sits at -0.88, indicating that retail grocery channels have absorbed price increases without triggering consumer panic buying or empty shelves.
  • Fertilizer & Logistics: Retail prices ticked higher across five of eight major fertilizer products in mid-November, accompanied by warnings regarding foreign supply reliance for sulfur. Port negotiations at Los Angeles and Long Beach introduce potential export and shipping frictions, but freight container overcapacity and current anchorage queues (8 vessels) have prevented acute domestic bottlenecks.

Household Guidance

With zero firsthand hoarding reports and stable domestic retail inventories, maintaining a baseline emergency pantry—consisting of two weeks of shelf-stable food, water, and regular prescription medications—remains appropriate. No emergency stockpiling is warranted at this time.

What would change this view

  • Sustained Strait of Hormuz tanker transits recovering above the 60 ships per day threshold, which would reduce global crude risk premiums.
  • A full work stoppage or strike at the Ports of Los Angeles and Long Beach, stranding import goods and agricultural exports.
  • A sudden drop in US commercial gasoline or distillate stocks below 10% under five-year seasonal averages.
