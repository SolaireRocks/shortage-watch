Overview and Current Assessment

The Shortage Pressure Index (SPI) sits at 61.4, remaining in the elevated band but showing modest easing from last month (down 0.9 points over the past 7 days and 6.3 points over the past 30 days). The primary locus of stress remains energy and transportation logistics. Middle East maritime disruptions continue to depress tanker traffic through the Strait of Hormuz—averaging just 3.9 daily transits against a typical baseline above 60—keeping global crude benchmarks elevated. Brent trades at $92.14 per barrel (+44.3% YoY) and WTI at $89.62 per barrel (+47.1% YoY).

Despite this backdrop, physical supply chains in the United States have avoided catastrophic failure. Domestic gasoline inventories stand at 210.8 million barrels, which is 0.6% above the 5-year seasonal average. Because physical supply continues to flow, short-term retail fuel disruption risks remain modest (4% over 1 week, 15% over 1 month). The main burden on households remains financial: pump prices average $3.995 per gallon for regular gasoline (+30.7% YoY) and diesel stands at $6.127 per gallon (+59.7% YoY). Heavy crack spreads and elevated freight rates continue to squeeze intermodal shipping and wholesale distribution, but physical stockouts across multi-state regions have not materialized.

Food, Natural Gas, and Fertilizer Outlook

  • Food (1-month risk: 1.5%; 3-month risk: 4.0%): International grains remain costly, with global wheat up 37.3% YoY to $317/mt and rice up 37.4% to $489/mt. Domestic food inflation rose a modest 0.5% in October, primarily driven by retail meat and produce. However, contiguous US drought coverage is 6.4 percentage points below its 5-year average, and grocery supply networks show no acute stockouts or purchase rationing.
  • Natural Gas (1-month risk: 1.5%; 3-month risk: 4.0%): Domestic natural gas fundamentals are calm, with the hard data index at -0.04. Storage levels and production heading into winter continue to protect against widespread residential curtailments under typical seasonal weather.
  • Fertilizer (1-month risk: 1.5%; 3-month risk: 4.0%): Retail fertilizer prices were mixed during the first week of November. While prices remain elevated compared to 2025, upstream application availability is currently sufficient.

Practical Recommendations

Household guidance remains at baseline. Standard emergency pantry reserves (two weeks of food, drinking water, and essential medicines) are sufficient. Given persistent diesel costs above $6 per gallon, keeping vehicle fuel tanks at or above half full provides a sensible, low-friction buffer against unexpected regional delivery hiccups.

What would change this view

  • A recovery in Strait of Hormuz transits toward normal levels (exceeding 60 vessels daily) would substantially reduce medium-term fuel and fertilizer disruption probabilities.
  • A sudden drop in US commercial gasoline inventories below 200 million barrels or regional refinery outages would sharply raise short-term fuel outage probabilities.
  • Major retail grocery chains enacting widespread purchase limits on dry goods or staple grains would require an immediate upward revision to food shortage probabilities.
