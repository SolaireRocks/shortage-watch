Current Situation

The composite Disease Index registers at 76.9, placing overall disease pressure in the elevated band. The primary driver is a rapid, early-season acceleration of seasonal influenza across the United States. Weekly nationwide influenza hospitalizations surged 81% over three weeks to 11,792 admissions, running more than 8,400 admissions above historical averages for late November. Clinical lab flu test positivity sits at 11.76%, while night coughing monitored via Sleep Cycle has risen to 9.37% of users. COVID-19 and RSV metrics show modest seasonal gains but remain below their respective multi-year historical averages.

In Ohio, trends parallel the national surge. Hospitalizations for influenza rose 126% over three weeks to 235 per week, and lab positivity reached 9.4%. Measles also remains elevated above 2025 levels in Ohio (238 cases year-to-date vs. 32 last year).

Pandemic Risk Assessment

Pandemic probabilities remain pinned close to historical base rates (0.1% at 1 week, 0.4% at 1 month, and 5.5% over 1 year). Although the World Health Organization reported three confirmed human H5N1 cases with one fatality among agricultural workers in Washington State, these cases stem from direct poultry contact. There is no evidence of secondary human-to-human transmission. Total US respiratory deaths (0.897 per 100,000) and overall acute respiratory emergency visits (14.48%) remain below seasonal benchmarks, confirming that current hospital strain reflects an early seasonal flu curve rather than an unmanaged novel outbreak.

What to Watch

Key indicators to monitor include whether the rapid growth in seasonal influenza admissions begins to strain regional hospital capacity, any genomic evidence of adaptive mutations or cluster spread in avian influenza cases, and whether winter COVID-19 or RSV waves compound current flu activity.

What would change this view

  • Cluster of human H5N1 cases with no direct animal or dairy exposure, indicating person-to-person spread.
  • A sharp, unseasonal spike in total acute respiratory distress deaths well above baseline.
