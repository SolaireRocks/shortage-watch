The overall Economy risk index sits at 23.6, firmly within the normal band and down 2.3 points over the past week. Core macroeconomic indicators show solid ongoing resilience, with virtually no sign of an impending contraction in the immediate future.

Financial conditions remain loose, as demonstrated by the Chicago Fed National Financial Conditions Index standing at -0.459. High-yield credit spreads, while exhibiting a minor stress score of 0.95, are modest at 3.34 points, well below levels associated with credit crunches. Meanwhile, the Treasury yield curve (10-year minus 3-month) is positively sloped at 0.68 points, and the equity market volatility index (VIX) is subdued at 19.46. Real-time electrical grid demand after weather adjustments is slightly above expectations (+0.348%), reflecting steady commercial and industrial operations.

Critically, the real-time Sahm rule indicator registers at 0.0 points, well below the 0.5 threshold that defines the onset of a labor-market-driven recession. Near-term recession risks over the next 1 to 3 months are very low (0.3% to 3.0%), closely matching prediction market consensus (around 2.1% across Polymarket and Kalshi). The 1-year probability is estimated at 24%, slightly above the historical baseline rate of 15% to account for broader medium-term cyclical uncertainty, aligned with crowd markets pricing around 24.3%.

For households, current conditions warrant no special defensive measures. Standard budgeting and routine savings practices remain appropriate.

What would change this view

  • A sudden rise in the real-time Sahm rule toward or past the 0.5 mark indicating rapid labor market softening.
  • A sharp widening in high-yield credit spreads past 5.0 points alongside a spike in the VIX volatility index.
  • A sustained contraction in weather-adjusted US electricity grid demand signaling broad industrial slowdown.
