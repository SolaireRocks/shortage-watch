Current Situation

The Conflict and Geopolitics index sits at 47.3 as of November 11, remaining firmly within the normal band with zero change over the past 7 and 30 days. Hard data indicates that global conflict pressures remain baseline: the latest worldwide conflict fatalities monitored by the Uppsala Conflict Data Program (UCDP) logged 13,286 deaths for September, corresponding to a benign stress score of -0.07 standard deviations.

Concurrently, critical maritime chokepoints reflect ongoing regional containment. IMF PortWatch data through November 8 shows Strait of Hormuz commercial vessel activity severely depressed, recording 3.0 transits on the day and a 7-day moving average of 3.9 transits per day.

Why These Probabilities

The metric for reopening the Strait of Hormuz requires 7-day average transits to surpass 60 vessels daily. With current flows below 4 ships per day, near-term restoration remains highly improbable. Polymarket pricing assigns just a 2.1% probability of traffic normalizing by November 30, closely aligned with our 1-month forecast of 2.5%. Long-term reopening chances gradually rebound toward historical baselines, reaching 17% at 3 months and 53% at 1 year.

For a new conflict shock—defined strictly as a newly initiated war or blockade involving a top-five exporter of energy, fertilizer, or grain—probabilities remain anchored to historical base rates (11% over 12 months). While the active conflict in Iran persists, it represents an existing baseline engagement rather than a new qualifying disruption.

What to Watch

Key indicators to monitor include PortWatch transit tracking through the Strait of Hormuz for any incremental rebound in vessel volume, alongside any signs of conflict expansion into adjacent Persian Gulf energy corridors.

What would change this view

  • A sustained rise in IMF PortWatch daily Strait of Hormuz transits above 20 to 30 ships per day would indicate progress toward reopening and sharply raise 1-to-3-month odds.
  • New blockades, kinetic strikes, or open warfare expanding into critical trade corridors or involving additional top energy or agricultural exporters.
