Current Situation

Overall domestic shortage pressure remains in the normal range, with the Shortage Pressure Index (SPI) registering at 59.1, down 11.9 points over the past week. Hard data across US supply chains demonstrates resilience against severe physical disruption, even as headline input costs and transportation fees remain high. Retail fuel markets saw slight price moderation, with national average diesel easing below $6.00 to $5.937 per gallon, though it remains 53.5% higher year-over-year. US regular gasoline averages $3.805 per gallon, and commercial gasoline inventories sit 2.6% above their five-year seasonal average, buffering consumer supplies against near-term pump dry-outs.

Why These Probabilities

Severe shortages in the United States remain historically rare events, requiring widespread physical unavailability or extreme price velocity (such as a 25% pump price jump in four weeks or multi-state fuel stockouts). While Brent crude ($92.94/bbl) and WTI crude ($89.53/bbl) reflect ongoing geopolitical premiums—reinforced by Strait of Hormuz maritime transits stagnating at just 2.3 ships per day—actual domestic availability remains intact. Distillate stocks are only 6.8% below their seasonal norm, keeping the 1-week fuel shortage probability low at 4.0%, while structural deficits and reduced Strategic Petroleum Reserve inventories (276.36 million barrels) justify an elevated 1-year fuel risk of 45.0%.

In the food sector, acute international pressures and high global prices (HRW wheat at $317/mt and Thai rice at $489/mt) contribute to elevated farm-level and grocery logistics costs. Spot freight rates have spiked over 50% year-over-year, and secondary cost pressures like the USPS 11% fuel surcharge are impacting retailers. However, these issues manifest as price inflation rather than physical stockouts. With zero consumer hoarding detected (hoarding signal z = 0.0) and a balanced food fear gap (-0.07), near-term food shortage risk remains negligible at 0.3% over one week and 1.5% over one month.

Natural gas and fertilizer domains are similarly quiet in physical metrics, with hard data indices near zero (natgas D = -0.02; fertilizer D = 0.01). While retail prices for five of eight major fertilizers nudged upward, dealer distribution networks remain functional heading toward the winter season.

What to Watch

Over the coming weeks, key monitoring points include negotiations surrounding potential labor actions at the Port of Los Angeles and Long Beach, winter heating demand trajectories, and commercial distillate inventory draws. In the energy sector, any shifts in Gulf transit volumes or unexpected refinery outages represent the primary vectors capable of translating upstream tightness into domestic household shortages.

What would change this view

  • A rapid normalization of maritime traffic through the Strait of Hormuz toward the historical 60 ships per day baseline, which would sharply lower fuel disruption probabilities.
  • A major pipeline outage, severe winter freeze, or sustained port labor strike at LA-Long Beach that causes physical distribution failures at US retail outlets.
  • Evidence of consumer panic buying or national grocery chains instituting purchase limits on core staple goods.
