Current Infrastructure Posture

The overall infrastructure hazard index sits at 59.9, remaining within the normal band (under 60) after modest declines over the past 7 and 30 days (-3.8 and -1.8 points, respectively). Natural hazard indicators remain relatively quiet: the daily maximum planetary K-index registered at 1.67, indicating minimal geomagnetic stress on regional electrical grids, and global seismic activity shows 4 earthquakes of magnitude 6.5 or higher over the past 30 days, within normal ranges.

Cyber Activity and Physical Networks

The primary area of elevated operational friction remains cyber threats. The Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA) added 38 newly exploited vulnerabilities to its catalog in the past 30 days (stress score 2.14), alongside one industrial control system vulnerability over the trailing 90 days. Joint advisories from the FBI and CISA have warned critical infrastructure operators about active intrusions targeting industrial refrigeration and cold-chain facilities. Concurrently, maritime shipping operator Maersk disclosed a cyber incident affecting a legacy system amid scattered attacks on global logistics networks. However, neither event has triggered functional failures across primary transportation corridors or fuel supplies.

Risk Outlook

The benchmark threshold for this briefing requires an event that stops a major pipeline, a top-10 US port, a regional electrical grid, or a national healthcare claims switch for at least three consecutive days. Historically, such events occur roughly once every four to five years (a 20% annual baseline). Today's forward probabilities are maintained at 1.5% for 1 week, 5% for 1 month, and 26% for 1 year. While opportunistic cyber probing remains steady, current systemic resilience and the absence of acute physical or space weather shocks keep near-term risk stable.

What would change this view

  • A confirmed ransomware disruption shutting down operations at a top-10 US container port or primary fuel distribution pipeline.
  • Severe space weather conditions reaching G4 or G5 intensity (Kp index of 8 or higher) threatening regional electric transmission grids.
  • Prolonged severe winter storm conditions creating widespread transmission freeze-offs comparable to Winter Storm Uri.
