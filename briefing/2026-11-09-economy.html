Current Assessment

The US economic risk environment remains categorized as normal, with the composite stress index standing at 32.9 out of 100. While short-term market turbulence has lifted equity volatility—the VIX rose to 28.18—underlying macroeconomic and credit fundamentals continue to show resilience.

Core Signals

Forecast Justification

Historical base rates imply approximately a 15% annual baseline probability of recession (averaging one downturn every 6 to 7 years). For the near horizons (1 week at 0.3%, 1 month at 1.3%, and 3 months at 3.5%), recession odds remain minimal and aligned with crowd predictions (3.2% over 3 months on Kalshi and Polymarket). At the 1-year horizon, probability is set at 24%, slightly above long-term base rates due to higher asset volatility and moderately tight real borrowing costs, closely tracking prediction market consensus of 24.8%.

What to Watch

Key triggers that would prompt an upward revision include sharp widening in high-yield spreads beyond 5.0 points, sustained contraction in commercial energy demand, or any significant upward revision in unemployment figures pushing the Sahm rule toward 0.5.

What would change this view

  • A sudden rise in the Sahm rule toward 0.5 alongside a surge in initial jobless claims.
  • High-yield credit spreads widening rapidly past 500 basis points accompanied by a sustained freeze in corporate credit issuance.
