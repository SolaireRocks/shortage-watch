Current Scarcity Assessment

The Shortage Pressure Index (SPI) sits at 65.6, placing national supply conditions in the elevated band. The primary source of ongoing pressure remains downstream energy and transportation logistics. US retail diesel remains high at $5.937 per gallon—up 53.5% year-over-year—despite recently slipping back below the $6 mark. Brent crude ($92.94/bbl) and WTI ($89.53/bbl) reflect sustained tightness, compounded by severely depressed shipping volume in the Persian Gulf, where Strait of Hormuz commercial transits linger at 2.3 daily.

Crucially for the consumer, high energy prices have not translated into widespread physical fuel shortages. US gasoline stocks stand at 215.9 million barrels, 2.6% above their 5-year average for mid-November, providing a significant physical buffer against station outages. While distillate inventories remain 6.8% below seasonal averages, domestic refining operations continue to satisfy core retail demand. Consequently, while the cumulative 1-year probability of a qualifying multi-state fuel disruption is elevated at 45%, the 1-week probability is restrained at 4.0%.

Food and Fertilizer Outlook

Staple grocery distribution remains resilient. Global grain markets continue to register elevated benchmark pricing—with World Bank data indicating wheat up 37.3% YoY to $317/mt and rice up 37.4% YoY to $489/mt. Domestically, farmdoc daily notes below-trend yields across Illinois, and freight tender rejections have more than doubled from year-ago levels, exerting upward pressure on wholesale logistics. However, retail grocery shelves remain stocked, and national panic buying is absent (the firsthand hoarding z-score is 0.0). Food shortage odds remain low near base rates at 0.3% over 1 week and 1.5% over 1 month.

Fertilizer retail prices saw slight increases in mid-November across five of eight tracked categories, and defense officials have highlighted potential strategic vulnerabilities surrounding upstream sulfur and feedstocks. With hard data indices near flat (D = 0.01), immediate supply remains accessible outside active spring application windows, keeping 1-month disruption probability at 1.5%.

Household Guidance

Stocking guidance remains at baseline. Households should maintain a typical emergency pantry containing 7 to 14 days of non-perishable food and potable water. No extraordinary grocery stockpiling is warranted. As a precautionary measure, drivers should maintain fuel tanks at or above half capacity to smooth out localized freight bottlenecks and regional price volatility heading into the winter holiday travel period.

What would change this view

  • A sudden recovery of Strait of Hormuz tanker transits above 60 ships per day would sharply lower medium-term fuel and fertilizer probabilities.
  • A formal strike declaration and sustained shutdown at the Ports of Los Angeles and Long Beach that stalls agricultural distribution would trigger increases in near-term food and supply-chain risk estimates.
  • A sharp decline in domestic refinery output dropping US gasoline stocks below -5% of seasonal norms would raise 1-month fuel disruption odds.
