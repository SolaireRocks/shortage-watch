Risk Assessment

Pandemic probabilities remain pegged strictly near historical base rates, scaling from 0.1% over the next week to 5.5% over the full year. Under our definition, a pandemic event requires a WHO declaration of a Public Health Emergency of International Concern (PHEIC) for a novel pathogen or confirmed, sustained human-to-human transmission of H5N1 in the United States. Routine seasonal surges in known pathogens such as influenza A and SARS-CoV-2 do not satisfy these criteria. Without evidence of novel antigenic shifts, human-adapted avian influenza lineages, or severe uncontained outbreaks abroad, the probability of an emerging pandemic event remains low and steady.

What to Watch

Over the coming weeks, attention centers on the steepness of the seasonal influenza trajectory and whether hospital systems face compound winter respiratory pressure. On the pandemic front, surveillance continues to focus on genetic sequencing from human avian flu exposures, reports of mammal-to-mammal transmission chains, and international alerts for unexplained severe respiratory clusters.
