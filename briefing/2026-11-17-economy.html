Current Conditions

Macroeconomic risk remains low, with the composite economy index reading 21.4 out of 100, firmly in the normal band. The index has declined 2.5 points over the past week and 11.1 points over the past month, reflecting broad stabilization across credit, labor, and industrial activity metrics.

Key real-economy indicators show solid health. The real-time Sahm rule stands at 0.0 points as of October 2026, far below the 0.5-point threshold that historically marks the start of a recession. Weather-adjusted US grid demand over the past four weeks ran 0.61% above expected levels, signaling steady commercial and industrial throughput. In financial markets, the Chicago Fed National Financial Conditions Index printed at -0.462 as of November 6, indicating conditions remain looser than historical averages. High-yield corporate credit spreads are at 3.23 percentage points, and the Cboe VIX closed at 19.3, indicating typical equity market fluctuations without widespread systemic stress.

Forecast Justification

We assess the near-term risk of a US recession beginning within one week at 0.3%, one month at 1.3%, and three months at 3.0%. These near-term figures run below the baseline rate (3.97% for three months) because high-frequency labor and credit data show no signs of imminent contraction. Further out, the probability increases to 11% at six months and 24% over the next full year, aligning closely with prediction market consensus (Kalshi and Polymarket average 26.3% over one year) and slightly elevated relative to the long-term annual baseline of 15% due to late-cycle uncertainty.

Key Indicators to Monitor

We continue tracking monthly unemployment and Sahm rule updates for unexpected labor softness, weekly high-yield credit spreads for signs of refinancing distress, and electricity demand patterns for real-time changes in economic momentum.

What would change this view

  • A rapid rise in the Sahm rule toward 0.5 indicating labor market deterioration.
  • Spike in high-yield credit spreads past 5.0 points signaling significant tightening in corporate credit.
  • Chicago Fed financial conditions flipping positive and tightening sharply over several consecutive weeks.
