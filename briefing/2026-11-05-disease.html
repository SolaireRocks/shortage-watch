Current Situation

The Disease risk index is currently elevated at 65.1, up 1.6 points over the past seven days. This elevated reading is primarily driven by macro-environmental and seasonal baseline factors rather than an emerging pandemic. An ongoing, strengthening El Niño event (|ONI| at 2.46°C) continues to dominate environmental stress metrics, while seasonal respiratory activity is beginning its expected autumn rise. Emergency department visits attributed to influenza increased to 1.86% of all visits as of October 31 (stress score 2.55). Meanwhile, SARS-CoV-2 metrics remain stable, with wastewater viral activity at a moderate 4.07 median WVAL and COVID-19 emergency department visits at 0.47%.

Assessment of Pandemic Probabilities

Despite the elevated composite index, the probability of a true pandemic-level event remains tightly anchored to historical base rates: 0.1% over the next week, 0.4% over one month, 1.3% over three months, 2.6% over six months, and 5.5% over the full year. Under our definition, a pandemic event requires a WHO Public Health Emergency of International Concern (PHEIC) for a novel pathogen or confirmed sustained human-to-human transmission of H5N1 in the United States. While preprint outbreak literature shows a slight uptick (0.316 log ratio over 90-day baseline), no novel pathogen clusters or sustained human transmission chains of high-consequence pathogens have been detected.

What We Are Watching

We continue to monitor CDC surveillance data for seasonal flu acceleration and any genetic markers indicating mammalian adaptation in H5N1 avian influenza samples. Key trigger points that would materially raise pandemic odds include confirmed human-to-human transmission chains among dairy or poultry workers, sudden jumps in unexplained acute respiratory distress hospitalizations, or international declarations of an emerging pathogen threat by the WHO.

What would change this view

  • Laboratory confirmation of sustained human-to-human transmission of avian influenza H5N1 within the United States.
  • Declaration of a Public Health Emergency of International Concern (PHEIC) by the World Health Organization for a novel viral pathogen.
  • Widespread, unlinked clusters of severe novel respiratory illness causing sudden surges in national ICU or emergency admissions.
