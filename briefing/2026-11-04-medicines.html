Current Supply Assessment

Overall national pharmaceutical scarcity remains in the normal range. The Pharmaceutical Scarcity Index (PSI) registers at 35.3, with active shortages well below historical peaks. As of the close of Q3 2026, the American Society of Health-System Pharmacists (ASHP) tracked 225 active drug shortages, substantially below the qualifying event threshold of 300 that marked the height of the 2023–2024 supply disruption. The FDA reports 69 active shortages, with generic price-shock breadth holding low at 0.8%.

Forecast Drivers and Telehealth Horizon

Probabilities for broad pharmaceutical disruptions track close to long-run historical baselines (0.4% at 1 week, rising to 21% over 1 year) due to absence of acute systemic shocks.

This spike is driven almost entirely by administrative policy: federal telemedicine flexibilities permitting remote prescribing of Schedule II medications expire on December 31, 2026—just 57 days away. Although regulatory agencies have extended these flexibilities four times previously, a failure to issue another temporary extension or publish a permanent special registration rule would constitute a lapse under our event criteria.

What to Watch

Over the next 60 days, monitor Federal Register notices for action from the DEA and HHS regarding telehealth prescribing rules.

What would change this view

  • ASHP active shortages climbing back toward 280 or multiple major generic oral antibiotic classes entering sudden shortage.
  • The DEA and HHS publishing a fifth extension or a permanent special registration rule for controlled substance telemedicine prescribing before December 31, 2026.
