Economic Overview

The domestic economy continues to track in healthy territory. The overall composite stress score sits at 25.7, firmly within the normal range. While financial market indicators have shown moderate churn—the CBOE Volatility Index (VIX) touched 24.56 and high-yield credit spreads ticked up to 3.52 points—real-side activity data shows no signs of acute distress.

Labor Market and Leading Indicators

The labor market remains the primary anchor of expansion. Initial unemployment claims printed at 203,000 for the week of November 14, reflecting historically low layoff activity and a strong labor backdrop. Correspondingly, the real-time Sahm rule indicator stands at 0.0 points, far removed from the 0.5-point trigger that defines a cyclical inflection. Broader financial plumbing also appears healthy: the Chicago Fed National Financial Conditions Index registered -0.478, indicating that credit and liquidity channels remain generally loose.

Outlook and Probabilities

Near-term recession risk remains subdued, assessed at 0.3% over one week and 1.3% over one month, slightly below long-term baseline rates. The 3-month probability is positioned at 2.5%, roughly in line with prediction market pricing (1.95%). Looking outward to 12 months, recession risk is assessed at 26%, accounting for late-cycle uncertainty and aligned with crowd aggregates (28%).

What to Watch

For households, no immediate defensive adjustments are required beyond typical sound financial habits. We continue to monitor weekly jobless claims for unexpected spikes, credit spreads for institutional funding strains, and energy consumption patterns via normalized electrical grid demand.

What would change this view

  • A sustained rise in initial jobless claims above 260,000 over a four-week stretch.
  • A sharp jump in the Sahm rule metric toward the 0.5-point benchmark.
  • A rapid tightening of credit conditions with high-yield credit spreads widening beyond 5.0 points.
