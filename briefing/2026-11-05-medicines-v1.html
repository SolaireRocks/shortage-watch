Current Supply Dynamics

National pharmaceutical supply chains continue to operate within normal historical ranges. The Pharmaceutical Scarcity Index registers at 35.3, well below the elevated threshold of 60. According to the latest American Society of Health-System Pharmacists (ASHP) data from Q3 2026, active shortages numbered 225—elevated relative to pre-2018 baselines, but significantly improved from the peak of 323 reached in early 2024. FDA active shortages currently stand at 69, with only one new shortage (pentostatin injection) recorded in the past 90 days. While hospital pharmacies continue to navigate localized friction in oncology and contrast media, widespread generic oral medication classes remain available.

Outlook and Household Watchpoints

For the general medicine supply, probabilities remain near historical baselines. Households with members receiving Schedule II medications exclusively via telehealth should monitor DEA announcements through November and December or consult their prescriber about arranging an in-person evaluation if rules are not updated.

What would change this view

  • The DEA and HHS publishing a fifth extension or a final rule creating a permanent special registration pathway for telemedicine prescribing of Schedule II medications before December 31, 2026.
  • ASHP active shortages rapidly rebounding toward 300 or a high-volume generic oral suspension (e.g., amoxicillin) entering nationwide shortage.
