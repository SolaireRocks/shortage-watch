Current Conditions

The infrastructure risk index registers at 71.4, remaining in the elevated band following a 9.0-point rise over the last week. The primary contributors to today's elevated stress are elevated cyber vulnerability exploitation and a minor geomagnetic disturbance. The Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA) has tracked 32 actively exploited vulnerabilities over the trailing 30 days, including one targeting industrial control systems. In space weather, NOAA's planetary K-index touched 5.0, corresponding to a minor G1 geomagnetic storm. Crucially, Kp 5 presents negligible operational hazard to bulk power grids, which typically face stress only at severe levels (Kp 8 or higher).

Recent reporting highlights ongoing cyber pressure against critical sectors, including ransomware groups targeting healthcare and oil entities, as well as a legacy system incident at shipping firm Maersk. However, these reports concurrently reflect international law enforcement takedowns and extraditions rather than systemic, uncontained operational failures.

Assessment and Horizon

Historically, events causing multi-day shutdowns of critical US infrastructure—such as the 2021 Colonial Pipeline outage, Winter Storm Uri, or the 2024 Change Healthcare disruption—occur roughly once every four to five years (a baseline 1-year rate around 20%). Because current indicators reflect background cyber and environmental strain rather than an active outage, probabilities remain held at 1.5% over the coming week, 5% over one month, and 26% over the full year.

What to Watch

Key indicators to monitor include any escalation in space weather toward major or severe geomagnetic storms (Kp 8+), early winter weather patterns threatening regional generation and fuel supplies, or confirmed disruptive cyberattacks penetrating operational technology at major ports, transmission hubs, or fuel pipelines.

What would change this view

  • A severe geomagnetic storm reaching Kp 8 or higher (G4/G5) that triggers grid voltage irregularities or transformer alarms.
  • Uncontained cyber intrusion into operational technology of a major pipeline, regional electric transmission operator, or major container port terminal.
  • A severe multi-region winter storm forecast threatening power grid adequacy or natural gas delivery.
