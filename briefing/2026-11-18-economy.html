Economic Outlook

The overall economic stress index sits at a calm 26.0 out of 100, firmly in the normal range. Fundamental activity indicators and credit metrics continue to reflect stable macroeconomic conditions, leaving the probability of a near-term downturn quite low.

Key Indicators and Drivers

Labor market stability remains the primary pillar holding recession risks down. The real-time Sahm rule indicator registers at 0.0 points, far below the 0.5 threshold that historically flags the onset of an economic contraction. Complementing this, broader financial conditions remain supportive of growth: the Chicago Fed National Financial Conditions Index is at -0.478, indicating looser financial conditions than historical averages, while the yield curve spread between the 10-year and 3-month Treasury yields is comfortably positive at +0.77 points.

While market volatility indicators show mild caution—the VIX index stands at 22.91 and high-yield credit spreads sit at 3.34 points—these readings remain within typical ranges during expansionary periods. Industrial activity also shows resilience, with weather-adjusted electricity demand tracking 0.63% above expected levels over the past four weeks.

Forecast and What to Watch

Short-term recession probabilities remain pinned near historical baseline levels, with a 1-month probability of 1.3% and a 3-month probability of 3.0%, closely tracking prediction market anchors. Over the 1-year horizon, recession risk is estimated at 24%, reflecting the cumulative baseline possibility of cyclical slowing over twelve months.

For US households, standard financial prudence applies. Key watchpoints include any sustained weakening in payrolls that pushes the Sahm rule upward, or a sudden tightening in corporate credit markets.

What would change this view

  • A rapid rise in the real-time Sahm rule toward 0.5 points, signalling broad labour market softening.
  • A sharp widening of high-yield credit spreads past 5.0 points accompanied by a reversal into tight financial conditions.
