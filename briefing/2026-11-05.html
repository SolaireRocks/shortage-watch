Current Shortage Risk Assessment

The Shortage Pressure Index (SPI) sits at 64.4, placing the overall supply environment in the elevated band. Today's hard data continues to reflect an asymmetric distribution of stress: petroleum and transportation fuels are bearing the brunt of systemic strain, while domestic natural gas, fertilizer, and core food grocery supplies remain largely functional and well within baseline historical operational tolerances.

Fuel and Logistics Remain the Focal Point

Crude benchmarks remain sharply above year-ago levels, with Brent trading at $107.03/bbl (+62.7% year-over-year) and WTI at $99.64/bbl (+61.3% year-over-year). The domestic buffer remains thin, as the US Strategic Petroleum Reserve stands at 282,184 thousand barrels, down 31.1% from this time last year. At the consumer level, the strain is most apparent in distillate fuels: retail diesel is averaging $6.292/gal (+67.7% YoY), while regular retail gasoline is at $4.153/gal (+37.6% YoY).

These sustained fuel prices continue to squeeze commercial carriers. Freight indicators show contracting capacity and rising spot rates, prompting operational strain among truckload carriers and logistics providers like FedEx and Amazon. However, while freight costs are elevated and individual carriers report margin compression, physical diesel and gasoline delivery to retail stations has not crossed the threshold into widespread multi-state run-outs. The probability of a qualifying fuel disruption within 1 month remains at 15%, reflecting ongoing vulnerability rather than an acute nationwide collapse.

Food, Heating, and Fertilizer Outlets

Domestic food supply chains remain resilient. While global agricultural commodities exhibit elevated benchmarks—World wheat is at $317.0/mt (+37.3% YoY) and Thai 5% rice is at $489.0/mt (+37.4% YoY)—the broad FAO Food Price Index is up only 5.8% YoY. Crucially, domestic retail shelves remain stocked, with zero reports of widespread purchase rationing or sudden grocery shelf clearing. The 1-month risk of a domestic food event is estimated at 1.5%.

Natural gas hard data index values (-0.04) show no residential heating disruptions heading into late autumn. Fertilizer prices saw minor late-October upticks in products such as UAN28, but broad domestic supplies remain sufficient for seasonal needs.

Household Guidance

Household posture remains at baseline. Standard emergency pantry reserves (two weeks of food and drinking water) are adequate. Given sustained diesel volatility, maintaining vehicle fuel tanks above half-capacity is a sensible, low-friction measure to hedge against local distributor price swings.

What would change this view

  • A sustained reopening of the Strait of Hormuz above 60 daily transits, which would rapidly de-escalate crude oil and diesel crack spreads.
  • Major domestic refinery shutdowns or pipeline outages driving regional pump outages or pushing retail fuel prices up more than 25% over a 4-week window.
  • Severe early winter freeze events causing widespread Henry Hub spot price spikes above twice their 1-year median.

What to do

  • Prepare Fuel (gasoline, diesel)
    Keep vehicle tanks at least half full to avoid spot price swings and guard against isolated delivery lags caused by elevated trucking costs.
