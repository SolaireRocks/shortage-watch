Current Situation

The Shortage Pressure Index (SPI) is at 58.1, sitting inside the normal range (under 60) and continuing its gradual retreat over the past 30 days (-7.6 points). The hoarding signal is at zero, with no verified reports of domestic panic buying or empty shelves in staple goods across US regions.

Domain Assessment

  • Fuel and Energy: Fuel remains the most elevated domain, driven by high crude benchmarks (Brent at $92.94/bbl, up 47.2% year-over-year) and expensive retail diesel ($5.695/gal, up 48.7% year-over-year). Strait of Hormuz shipping volumes remain constrained at 2.7 ships per day compared to the normal 60. However, physical availability for passenger vehicles is well supported: US commercial gasoline inventories stand at 215.9 million barrels, 2.6% above the five-year seasonal average. Because refinery distribution channels remain operational, the probability of widespread retail station run-outs over the next week is low (4.0%), while medium-term odds (28.0% over three months) reflect broader geopolitical fragility.
  • Food: Grocery supply chains are stable. While global wheat ($317.0/mt) and rice ($489.0/mt) have risen roughly 37% year-over-year, and refrigerated truckload rejections have doubled heading into the holiday season, these pressures represent transport friction and cost inflation rather than physical domestic food deficits. The odds of qualifying supply disruptions or national grocery purchase limits remain remote (0.3% over one week, 4.0% over three months).
  • Natural Gas and Fertilizer: Natural gas markets are balanced entering winter, with hard data indicators neutral (D = 0.01). Fertilizer discussions have grown noisy due to policy debates over sulfur and Chinese trade dependencies, driving sentiment up (S = 1.71), but domestic retail availability and physical fundamentals (D = 0.01) show no immediate distribution blockage.

Upstream Threats

The disease tracker is elevated at 74.9 due to an early-season spike in hospital influenza admissions and three confirmed farmworker H5N1 cases in Washington State. Because no sustained human-to-human transmission is present, supply chain operations remain unaffected. Broader infrastructure, cybersecurity, and macroeconomic conditions remain stable.

Household Guidance

Maintain a baseline emergency pantry with several days to two weeks of non-perishable food, water, and regular medications. No special purchasing, fuel hoarding, or inventory expansion is warranted under current conditions.

What would change this view

  • A rapid fall in US gasoline stocks below the five-year minimum range or unexpected domestic refinery outages.
  • Sustained normalization of maritime tanker transits through the Strait of Hormuz back above 60 vessels per day, which would ease global petroleum benchmarks.
  • A breakout of sustained human-to-human transmission of avian influenza in agricultural logistics or livestock hubs.
