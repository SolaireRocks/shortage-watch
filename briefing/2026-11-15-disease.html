Current Assessment

The national Disease risk index registers at 55.0, comfortably within the normal range, having eased by 2.0 points over the past week and 0.7 points over the trailing 30 days. Routine respiratory viral surveillance shows expected early autumn movements without evidence of an uncharacteristic epidemic or emerging pathogen threat.

Influenza-related emergency department visits stood at 2.22% as of November 7, showing a typical seasonal rise (stress score of 2.6). Simultaneously, SARS-CoV-2 metrics remain subdued: emergency visits for COVID-19 accounted for only 0.52% of visits, and wastewater viral activity remains moderate at a median level of 5.47. Beyond viral pathogens, oceanographic data confirms a strong El Niño pattern with an Oceanic Niño Index absolute value of 2.46°C; while significant for broad ecological and vector dynamics, this presents no acute near-term pandemic pressure.

Probability & Trajectory

Pandemic probabilities remain anchored close to historical baseline expectations: 0.1% over one week, 0.4% over one month, 1.3% over three months, 2.6% over six months, and 5.5% over the full year. Historically, novel pandemic declarations or sustained human transmissions of high-consequence threats such as avian influenza occur roughly once every two decades. Because no sustained human-to-human transmission of novel influenza or other pathogens has been detected, near-term probabilities remain extremely low.

What We Are Watching

We monitor genomic surveillance and CDC data streams for genetic reassortment or cluster transmissions of avian influenza A(H5N1) in agricultural and commercial settings. In addition, routine tracking focuses on whether concurrent seasonal influenza and respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) trajectories place uncharacteristic stress on acute healthcare facilities as winter approaches.

What would change this view

  • Confirmed cluster of human-to-human transmission of avian influenza A(H5N1) within the United States.
  • A declaration of a Public Health Emergency of International Concern (PHEIC) by the WHO for a novel viral pathogen.
  • A rapid surge in unexplained severe acute respiratory illness leading to regional hospital capacity strains.
