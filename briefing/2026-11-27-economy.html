Current Assessment

The broad economy remains in the normal range, with our overall composite stress index registering at 29.3, comfortably below warning thresholds. Core macroeconomic fundamentals show no immediate sign of an impending downturn. Most critically, the Real-time Sahm rule indicator reads 0.0 points, indicating that the unemployment rate has not experienced the upward momentum historically associated with the start of a recession.

Financial markets display localized turbulence, with the VIX elevated at 26.36 and high-yield credit spreads at 3.72 points. However, broader financial plumbing remains resilient. The Chicago Fed National Financial Conditions Index sits at -0.444, reflecting financial conditions that are looser than historical averages. Furthermore, the 10-year minus 3-month Treasury yield curve maintains a positive spread of 0.51 points, and weather-adjusted US grid electricity demand is running 1.05% above expected seasonal baselines, pointing to steady commercial and industrial activity.

Horizon Probabilities

Near-term recession risk remains virtually negligible: 0.3% over 1 week, 1.3% over 1 month, and 3.0% over 3 months, aligning tightly with prediction market consensus (Kalshi and Polymarket aggregate at 4.0% for the remainder of the year). Because economic momentum takes time to break down absent an acute systemic shock, immediate downside risks are minimal. Over longer horizons, cumulative risks revert toward baseline and crowd estimates, estimated at 11% over 6 months and 26% over 1 year (crowd anchor at 28.3%).

Household Takeaway

No emergency financial posture or defensive actions are required today. Households should maintain standard financial preparedness, such as regular emergency savings contributions and debt management, without postponing necessary discretionary purchases or adjustments.

  • High-yield credit spreads blowing out above 5.0 points alongside a sharp tightening in the Chicago Fed financial conditions index.
  • Re-inversion of the 10-year minus 3-month yield curve coupled with contraction in weather-adjusted electricity demand.
